Number words above **tjugo** (twenty) follow one simple pattern: **tjugo, trettio, fyrtio, femtio, sextio, sjuttio, åttio, nittio, hundra** — twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, hundred. To make a compound number, just glue the tens and ones together with no space: **tjugoett** (21), **trettiotvå** (32), **femtiofem** (55).

```example
Det kostar tjugo kronor. — It costs twenty kronor.
Jag har hundra kronor kvar. — I have a hundred kronor left.
```

Swedish money is counted in **kronor** (the plural of **krona** — the Swedish crown). When you shop, you ask about the **pris** (price) and whether something is **dyrt** (expensive) or **billigt** (cheap). Most places accept a **kort** (card), though a few smaller shops still prefer **kontanter** (cash). Keep your **kvitto** (receipt) in case you need to return something later.

```example
Kan jag betala med kort? — Can I pay by card?
Är det dyrt eller billigt? — Is that expensive or cheap?
```

This number range is also useful far beyond shopping — telling someone your age, giving a phone number, or reading prices on a restaurant menu all rely on the same 20-100 words. Practice combining them out loud: **fyrtiotre** (43), **sjuttionio** (79), **nittiosex** (96) — once the pattern clicks, every number up to a hundred is just a matter of gluing two familiar words together.
