Swedish has no single future tense — instead, four different constructions
let you signal exactly what *kind* of future you mean.

**Ska** signals a decision, promise or plan the speaker controls.

```example
Jag ska ringa dig. — I will call you. (I'm deciding to, right now)
Vi ska resa till Grekland i sommar. — We are going to travel to Greece this summer. (our plan)
```

**Kommer att** signals a prediction — something the speaker expects to
happen, often outside their control.

```example
Det kommer att regna. — It's going to rain.
Hon kommer att bli trött. — She's going to get tired.
```

**Tänker** signals a personal intention — weaker than "ska", more like "I'm
thinking of...".

```example
Jag tänker flytta. — I intend to move.
Vad tänker du göra i helgen? — What are you planning to do this weekend?
```

Finally, the **present tense** covers a scheduled or certain future — train
and bus timetables, fixed appointments.

```example
Bussen går klockan åtta. — The bus leaves at eight.
Filmen börjar klockan sju. — The movie starts at seven.
```

Put side by side, the four often overlap in translation but not in nuance:

```example
Jag ska städa lägenheten. — I will clean the apartment. (my decision)
Det kommer att bli fint väder imorgon. — The weather will be nice tomorrow. (prediction)
Jag tänker läsa mer svenska i år. — I intend to study more Swedish this year. (intention)
Tåget går klockan tio. — The train leaves at ten. (schedule)
```

There is no strict rule forcing one choice over another — native speakers
often could use "ska" or "tänker" for the same sentence — but each carries
its own shade of certainty, control and formality that is worth noticing as
you read and listen.
