A reflexive verb describes an action the subject does **to itself**. In Swedish this
uses a reflexive pronoun that changes with the person doing the action — it does not
stay "sig" for everyone.

| person | reflexive pronoun |
|---|---|
| jag (I) | mig |
| du (you) | dig |
| han/hon/den/det (he/she/it) | sig |
| vi (we) | oss |
| ni (you, pl.) | er |
| de (they) | sig |

Note that **sig** is used for both third person singular and third person plural — the
only place where two different persons share one form.

Here is the full pattern with one verb, **tvätta sig** (to wash oneself):

```example
Jag tvättar mig. — I wash myself.
Du tvättar dig. — You wash yourself.
Han/Hon tvättar sig. — He/She washes himself/herself.
Vi tvättar oss. — We wash ourselves.
Ni tvättar er. — You (pl.) wash yourselves.
De tvättar sig. — They wash themselves.
```

Many everyday verbs work this way. The verb itself doesn't change its meaning without
the reflexive pronoun — but the object does:

```example
Jag känner mig trött idag. — I feel tired today.
Han rakar sig varje morgon. — He shaves every morning.
Kan du sätta dig, tack? — Could you sit down, please?
Vi måste skynda oss! — We have to hurry!
Hon klär sig varmt på vintern. — She dresses warmly in winter.
```

A simple check: if the subject and the "object" of the action are the same person,
you need a reflexive pronoun that matches the subject — not "honom", "henne" or
"dem", which would mean the action is done to someone else entirely.
