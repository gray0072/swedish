**Som** is Swedish's all-purpose relative pronoun — it corresponds to English
"who", "which", or "that", and never changes form regardless of gender or
number.

```example
Mannen som bor här är min granne. — The man who lives here is my neighbor.
Boken som jag läste var bra. — The book that I read was good.
```

Word order after som follows the normal subordinate-clause pattern you
already know (BIFF applies here too if there's an inte).

The interesting rule is when **som can be dropped**. It depends on its
grammatical role inside the relative clause:

- If som is the **subject** of the relative clause, it **must stay**.
- If som is the **object** of the relative clause, it **can be omitted**.

```example
Mannen som bor här är min granne. — som is the subject of "bor" — cannot drop.
Boken (som) jag läste var bra. — som is the object of "läste" — can drop.
```

A simple test: look at what comes right after som. If a verb follows
immediately (som bor, som fick), som is doing the job of the subject and stays
mandatory. If a subject pronoun or noun follows instead (som jag läste, som
hon fick), som is the object of that clause and native speakers often drop it
in everyday speech.

```example
Kvinnan (som) jag träffade var trevlig. — The woman (that) I met was nice.
Arbetet som hon fick var bra. — The job that she got was good.
```

Note that even when som is droppable, keeping it is never wrong — it's simply
optional, mostly a matter of style. Dropping it when som is the subject,
however, produces an ungrammatical sentence, so when in doubt, keep som.
