Five small expressions carry most of everyday time-talk beyond the basic
prepositions.

**För … sedan** places something in the past, counting back from now — "…
ago":

```example
Jag flyttade hit för två år sedan. — I moved here two years ago.
Det var för länge sedan. — That was a long time ago.
```

**Sedan** alone, without **för**, marks a starting point that continues to
now — "since":

```example
Jag har bott här sedan 2020. — I've lived here since 2020.
```

**Redan** ("already") and **ännu** ("yet/still") are near-opposites, and
often appear as a pair in conversation — one confirming something happened
early, the other noting it hasn't happened at all.

```example
Jag är redan klar. — I'm already done.
Jag är inte klar ännu. — I'm not done yet.
Han har ännu inte svarat. — He hasn't answered yet.
```

Notice that **ännu** most often appears together with **inte** — "ännu inte"
— to mean "not yet".

Finally, **i tid** means "on time", arriving exactly when expected — don't
confuse it with **i tiden**, a similar-looking but different, more abstract
phrase you may meet later.

```example
Kom i tid till mötet. — Come on time for the meeting.
```

Put together, these words let you place events precisely along a timeline:
how long ago something happened, since when it's been true, whether it's
already done, still pending, or exactly on schedule.
