Two small families of adverbs let you fine-tune how strong something is, and
how often it happens. Both are easy to place once you know the rule.

**Degree adverbs** modify an adjective and stand directly in front of it,
telling you *how much*:

```example
Det är ganska varmt. — It is fairly warm.
Det är väldigt kallt. — It is very cold.
Det är alltför dyrt. — It is too expensive.
```

**Ganska** ("fairly, rather") is mild — a slight boost. **Väldigt** ("very")
is a strong, neutral intensifier. **Alltför** ("too, excessively") signals a
problem — not just "a lot", but "more than is good":

```example
Filmen var väldigt tråkig. — The film was very boring.
Provet var alltför svårt. — The test was too difficult.
```

**Frequency adverbs** — like **sällan** (rarely), **ibland** (sometimes), and
**ofta** (often, which you already know) — work differently: they describe
the whole sentence, not one word. That means they follow the same rule you
learned for **satsadverbial** (sentence adverbs) like **inte** and **alltid**:
in a main clause, they go **right after the finite verb**.

```example
Jag äter sällan kött. — I rarely eat meat.
Vi går ibland på bio. — We sometimes go to the cinema.
Hon är redan klar. — She is already ready. (redan behaves the same way)
```

Compare the two families side by side: degree adverbs glue onto the
adjective they describe, while frequency adverbs claim the fixed slot after
the finite verb, wherever the adjective or object happens to be:

```example
Han är ganska trött, men han tränar ofta. — He is fairly tired, but he trains often.
```

A handy check: if the word answers "how much/how strong?", it sits next to
an adjective (degree adverb). If it answers "how often?", it jumps to the
spot right after the finite verb (frequency adverb) — exactly like **inte**
and **alltid** from your earlier lesson on sentence adverbs.
