Swedish has two ways to talk about the past, and choosing the right one is
about meaning, not just grammar. The **perfect** (har/hade + supinum) links a
past action to the present — the result matters now, and often no specific
time is mentioned. The **preterite** simply reports something that happened
at a particular point in time.

```example
Jag har läst boken. — I have read the book. (it's done — the result counts now)
Jag läste boken igår. — I read the book yesterday. (a specific past moment)
```

```example
Vi har flyttat till Malmö. — We have moved to Malmö. (we live there now)
Vi flyttade till Malmö förra året. — We moved to Malmö last year. (when it happened)
```

A good test: if the sentence names *when* something happened (igår, förra
veckan, i somras, klockan åtta), use the preterite. If it answers *whether*
something happened at all, with the focus on the present result, use the
perfect.

```example
Har du sett den filmen? — Have you seen that movie? (ever, up to now)
Såg du den filmen i går kväll? — Did you watch that movie last night? (specific time)
```

Certain words are strong signals for each tense. **Redan** (already),
**aldrig** (never), **just nu** (right now) and **någonsin** (ever) usually
go with the perfect, since they describe experience without a fixed date.

```example
Hon har aldrig varit i Spanien. — She has never been to Spain.
Har du någonsin ätit surströmming? — Have you ever eaten surströmming?
```

Specific time words — **igår**, **förra veckan**, **i somras**, **klockan
åtta** — call for the preterite instead. Mixing them, as in *"Jag har läst
boken igår"*, is a common mistake: once a specific time is named, Swedish
switches to the preterite.

```example
Jag har rest till Norge. — I have traveled to Norway. (experience)
Vi reste till Norge i somras. — We traveled to Norway last summer. (specific time)
```
