A whole opinion, with a reason, fits into one simple pattern:

```example
Jag tycker att skolan är bra eftersom lärarna är trevliga. — I think the
school is good because the teachers are nice.
```

The first half, **jag tycker att [claim]**, states your opinion as a
subordinate clause after "att". The second half, **eftersom [reason]**,
gives the reason — also its own clause. Both halves follow ordinary
subordinate-clause word order.

```example
Jag tycker att svenska är svårt eftersom uttalet är annorlunda. — I think
Swedish is difficult because the pronunciation is different.
Jag tycker att det är roligt att laga mat. — I think cooking is fun.
```

Two more verbs give the same pattern a slightly different shade: **jag tror
att** (I believe/think, a guess rather than a firm opinion) and **jag anser
att** (I consider, a bit more formal).

```example
Jag tror att det blir bättre väder imorgon. — I think the weather will be
better tomorrow.
Jag anser att alla borde få gå i skolan. — I consider that everyone should
be able to go to school.
```

You can build a short opinion about almost anything this way — just plug in
a claim and a reason:

```example
Jag tycker att det är viktigt att äta frukost eftersom det ger energi. — I
think it's important to eat breakfast because it gives energy.
```

Keep the sentence to one claim and one reason — that's the whole point of
the pattern: a clear opinion in a single, simple sentence.
