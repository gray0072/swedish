Beyond simple hobbies, Swedish free time is often organised. Going to the **bio** (cinema) or **teater** (theatre) usually means checking the **repertoar** (programme) first and then **boka biljetter** (booking tickets) online.

```example
Ska vi gå på bio ikväll? — Shall we go to the cinema tonight?
Filmen har premiär nästa fredag. — The film premieres next Friday.
```

A **förestallning** (performance) takes place in a **salong** (auditorium), and if you're seeing something popular, the **publik** (audience) can be large. Notice how **gå på bio** simply means "to go to the cinema" — the preposition **på** is fixed here and doesn't change.

```example
Vi går på bio varje fredag. — We go to the cinema every Friday.
Föreställningen börjar klockan sju. — The performance starts at seven.
```

Organised sport works through a **förening** (club, association) — for example, an **idrottsförening** (sports club). Children and adults **anmäla sig** (sign up) to join, become **medlemmar** (members), and pay a yearly **medlemsavgift** (membership fee). A child who **spelar fotboll i en förening** (plays football in a club) usually **tränar** (trains) with a **tränare** (coach) a few times a week and plays a **match** (match) at the weekend.

```example
Min dotter spelar fotboll i en förening. — My daughter plays football in a club.
Vi tränar två gånger i veckan. — We train twice a week.
```

Understanding this small set of words helps you talk about weekly routines, join a local club, and follow announcements about performances and matches around town.
