Some Swedish verbs end in **-s** in their basic form without being passive at all.
There are two different reasons for this, and it helps to tell them apart.

**Reciprocal verbs** are built from an ordinary verb by adding **-s**, and they mean
"each other" or "one another". They come in pairs: one form for doing something *to*
someone, and the -s form for doing it *to each other*.

```example
träffa — to meet (someone)
träffas — to meet (each other)

se — to see
ses — to see each other

krama — to hug (someone)
kramas — to hug each other
```

```example
Vi träffas klockan sju. — We're meeting (each other) at seven.
Vi ses imorgon! — See you tomorrow!
Vännerna kramas när de möts. — The friends hug each other when they meet.
De skiljs åt vid stationen. — They part ways at the station.
```

**Deponent verbs** just happen to end in **-s** in every form — present, past and
supine. There is no matching non-s verb; the -s is simply part of the word.

```example
hoppas – hoppas – hoppades — hoppats (to hope)
trivas – trivs – trivdes — trivts (to feel at home)
minnas – minns – mindes — mints (to remember)
andas – andas – andades — andats (to breathe)
```

```example
Jag hoppas att det blir soligt imorgon. — I hope it will be sunny tomorrow.
Jag trivs bra i Sverige. — I feel at home in Sweden.
Jag minns inte hans namn. — I don't remember his name.
Andas lugnt. — Breathe calmly.
```

Notice that the infinitives **trivas** and **minnas** end in -s, but the everyday
present-tense forms you'll hear most often are **trivs** and **minns** — that's why
those two forms are often the first ones learners meet.

A useful test: if you can imagine a non-s version with a normal meaning ("träffa",
"se", "krama"), it's probably reciprocal. If there is no such normal version, it's a
deponent verb — just learn it as it is.
