Talking about work in Swedish starts with one key question: **Vad jobbar du som?** ("What do you work as?"). The answer usually names a **yrke** (profession) directly, without an article: *Jag är lärare*, not *Jag är en lärare* — Swedish drops the article for professions, unlike English.

```example
Vad jobbar du som? — What do you work as?
Jag är sjuksköterska. — I am a nurse.
```

Some common professions: **lärare** (teacher), **läkare** (doctor), **kock** (cook), **ingenjör** (engineer), **förare** (driver), **butiksbiträde** (shop assistant), **byggarbetare** (construction worker). If you're studying, you're a **student**; if you have no job right now, you're **arbetslös** (unemployed); if you've retired, you're a **pensionär** (pensioner) — these two are not the same thing, even though neither one works.

```example
Jag är student och jobbar deltid som kock. — I'm a student and work part time as a cook.
Han är arbetslös just nu. — He is unemployed right now.
```

The verb **jobba** ("to work") is the everyday word for going to your job: *Jag jobbar på ett sjukhus* ("I work at a hospital"). Your **jobb** (job) happens at your **arbetsplats** (workplace), where your **chef** (boss) is in charge of the team.

Once you know a handful of these words, you can hold a short conversation about work with almost anyone: ask what they do, say what you do, and mention where you do it.
