You already know a handful of preterite (past-tense) forms like **var**, **hade**, **gick** and **fick**. Now let's use a few more common ones, together with time words, to tell a short connected story about something that already happened.

```example
Igår åkte jag till stan. — Yesterday I went to town.
Jag träffade en vän. — I met a friend.
```

To link events in order, Swedish uses simple connectors: **först** (first), **sedan** (then), and **till slut** (in the end). Just like in English, they let you turn a list of separate sentences into one flowing account.

```example
Först åkte jag till jobbet. Sedan handlade jag mat. Till slut lagade jag mat hemma. — First I went to work. Then I did the shopping. In the end I cooked at home.
```

Two useful time markers for a whole story are **igår** (yesterday) and **i förra veckan** (last week) — they usually come first in the sentence, before the verb.

```example
I förra veckan träffade jag en gammal vän. — Last week I met an old friend.
Vi pratade länge om jobbet. — We talked for a long time about work.
```

For now, treat phrases like **jag åkte**, **jag träffade**, **jag handlade**, **jag lagade mat** and **jag tittade på tv** as ready-made blocks — you don't need to know every verb's full conjugation pattern yet, just recognise these as "I did X." Later in this level you'll learn the general rules for building the regular preterite from any verb; for now, this handful of phrases is enough to describe your yesterday or your last week in a few connected sentences.

```example
Igår tittade jag på tv hela kvällen. — Yesterday I watched TV all evening.
```
