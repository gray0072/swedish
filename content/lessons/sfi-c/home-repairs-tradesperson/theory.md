When something breaks at home, two short phrases describe most problems:

```example
Det läcker under diskbänken. — It's leaking under the sink.
Diskmaskinen är trasig. — The dishwasher is broken.
```

Once you know what's wrong, you call the right **hantverkare**
(tradesperson): a **rörmokare** for water and pipes, an **elektriker** for
anything electrical.

```example
Jag måste ringa en rörmokare. — I need to call a plumber.
Elektrikern kommer imorgon. — The electrician is coming tomorrow.
```

A dripping **kran** (tap) or a fuse that's blown are two of the most common
everyday problems:

```example
Kranen droppar hela natten. — The tap drips all night.
Ljuset funkar inte, proppen har gått. — The light doesn't work, the fuse has blown.
```

To arrange a visit, use **boka en hantverkare** and ask when they can come:

```example
Jag ska boka en hantverkare imorgon. — I'll book a tradesperson tomorrow.
När kan du komma och titta på det? — When can you come and take a look?
```

Before work starts, it's normal to ask for an **offert** — a price quote —
so there are no surprises on the bill.

```example
Kan du skicka en offert? — Can you send a quote?
```

Putting it together, a short call might sound like this:

```example
– Hej, det läcker under diskbänken. Kan du laga kranen?
– Ja, jag kan komma på torsdag. Reparationen tar en dag.
```
