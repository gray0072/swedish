Every month a Swedish household pays a handful of regular **räkningar** (bills): **hyra** (rent), **elräkning** (electricity bill), and often an **internetabonnemang** (internet subscription). Each bill arrives as a **faktura** (invoice), either on paper or in your bank's app.

```example
Jag måste betala hyran idag. — I have to pay the rent today.
Elräkningen kom med posten. — The electricity bill came in the mail.
```

Every invoice has a **förfallodag** — the due date by which the **belopp** (amount) must be paid. To pay in the bank's app or at a machine, you usually type in the **OCR-nummer**, a reference number that tells the receiver which invoice you're paying, plus the **bankgiro** or **plusgiro** account number.

```example
Vilket belopp ska jag betala? — What amount should I pay?
Förfallodagen är den 27:e. — The due date is the 27th.
```

Many people set the rent and other fixed bills on **autogiro** — an automatic direct debit that pays the bill from your account on the due date, so you never forget. If a bill is **försenad** (overdue), you may get a **påminnelseavgift** (reminder fee) added on top.

```example
Jag har hyran på autogiro. — I have the rent on autopay.
Räkningen är försenad, så jag fick en påminnelseavgift. — The bill is overdue, so I got a reminder fee.
```

Bills that arrive **månadsvis** (monthly) are easiest to plan for — some people try to **spara pengar** (save money) by comparing electricity providers when there's a **höjning** (price increase). Whichever way you pay, it's smart to keep the **kvitto** or a screenshot as proof.
