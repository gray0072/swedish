**Säkerhet** (safety) at a Swedish workplace usually starts with **skyddsutrustning** (safety equipment): a **hjälm** (helmet) for your head, **skyddsglasögon** (safety goggles) for your eyes, **skyddshandskar** (safety gloves) for your hands, and **skyddsskor** (safety shoes) for your feet. Outdoors, especially near traffic, you also wear a **reflexväst** (hi-vis vest) so drivers can see you.

```example
Ta på dig hjälmen innan du går in. — Put on the helmet before you go in.
Bär alltid reflexväst utomhus. — Always wear a hi-vis vest outdoors.
```

Signs matter too: a **varningsskylt** (warning sign) tells you about a **fara** (danger) nearby, and every building has a **nödutgång** (emergency exit) that should never be blocked. Near it, or in the staff room, you'll usually find a **brandsläckare** (fire extinguisher) and a **förbandslåda** (first-aid kit) for **första hjälpen** (first aid).

```example
Nödutgången är till vänster om dörren. — The emergency exit is to the left of the door.
Är någon skadad? — Is anyone injured?
```

If an **olycka** (accident) happens — someone is **skadad** (injured) or has a **skada** (injury) — the most important rule is to **rapportera** (report) it right away, even if it seems minor. Reporting helps the workplace fix the danger before it hurts someone else.

Remembering just a handful of words — **hjälm**, **olycka**, **rapportera**, **första hjälpen** — is often enough to understand safety instructions and act correctly if something goes wrong.
