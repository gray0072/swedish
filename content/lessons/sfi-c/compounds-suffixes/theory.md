Swedish builds new words in two very productive ways: joining two words
together, or adding a suffix.

**Compounds** simply put two nouns together as one word, with the stress on
the *first* part.

```example
brev + låda → brevlåda — letter + box → letterbox, mailbox
körkort + tillstånd → körkortstillstånd — driving licence + permit → learner's permit
```

Once joined, a compound behaves as one word with one gender (usually taken
from the last part) — you never write the two parts separately or with a
hyphen.

```example
Brevet ligger i brevlådan. — The letter is in the mailbox.
```

Three suffixes turn a verb or adjective into a new noun, and it's worth
recognising all three.

**-are** makes an "agent" noun — the person who does the verb:

```example
arbeta → arbetare — to work → worker
läsa → läsare — to read → reader
```

**-het** makes an abstract noun from an adjective — the quality itself:

```example
snäll → snällhet — kind → kindness
fri → frihet — free → freedom
skön → skönhet — beautiful → beauty
```

**-ning** makes an action/result noun from a verb:

```example
undersöka → undersökning — to examine → examination
utbilda → utbildning — to educate/train → education, training
```

Once you notice these patterns, you can often guess a new word's meaning
just from its root and its ending.
