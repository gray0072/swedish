Every workplace has a **schema** (schedule) that tells you your **arbetstid** (working hours) and your **arbetspass** (shift). At the start of the day you **börja** (start) jobbet, and at the end you **sluta** (finish) — both are group‑1 verbs.

```example
När börjar du jobbet? — When do you start work?
Jag slutar klockan fem. — I finish at five o'clock.
```

Two kinds of breaks matter in Swedish working life: the **rast** (a general break) and the **fikarast** — a short coffee break that's almost a national institution. A longer break for eating is the **lunchrast** (lunch break). If someone asks how long a break is, they say **"Hur lång är rasten?"**

```example
Vi tar en fikarast klockan tio. — We take a coffee break at ten o'clock.
Hur lång är rasten? — How long is the break?
```

Working with others means talking to your **kollega** (colleague) and sometimes your **chef** (boss). If you don't understand a task, the most useful phrase is **"Kan du hjälpa mig?"** ("Can you help me?"). You can make it more specific with **"Jag behöver hjälp med det här."** ("I need help with this.") If you simply want to ask something, say **"Jag har en fråga."** ("I have a question.")

At the end of a break, a colleague might say **"Vi ses efter rasten."** ("See you after the break.") And if someone is at work today, you can describe them as **närvarande** — present, at their post.

Putting it together: a normal day means checking the **schema**, working through your **arbetspass**, taking a **fikarast** with **kollegor**, and — whenever something is unclear — simply asking, **"Kan du hjälpa mig?"**
