You already know **"Ring 112!"** for emergencies. It's worth adding **"Ring en ambulans!"** ("Call an ambulance!") for a medical emergency, and knowing that the operator will likely ask **"Vad har hänt?"** ("What happened?") and **"Var hände det?"** ("Where did it happen?"). Being ready to **beskriva** (describe) the situation clearly and calmly helps the ambulans arrive faster.

```example
Ring en ambulans! — Call an ambulance!
Kan du beskriva vad som hände? — Can you describe what happened?
```

Not every problem is a life-threatening **akut** (urgent) situation, though. If you've been the victim of a **stöld** (theft) — your bike, your wallet — or you've lost important **dokument** (documents) like your **pass** (passport), you don't call 112. Instead, you make a **polisanmälan** (police report), usually online or at a police station.

```example
Jag vill anmäla en stöld. — I want to report a theft.
Mitt pass är borta. — My passport is gone.
```

To **anmäla** (report) something to the police, be ready to say what is **förlorad** (lost) or stolen, and roughly **när hände det** (when it happened). A police report is important even for lost documents — you'll often need it to get a replacement passport or ID.

So remember the split: **112** and **"Ring en ambulans!"** for emergencies where someone is hurt or in danger right now; a calm **polisanmälan** — **"Jag vill anmäla en stöld"** or **"Mitt pass är borta"** — for a theft or lost documents that already happened.
