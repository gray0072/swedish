In Sweden, **sopsortering** (waste sorting) is part of everyday life. Instead of throwing everything into one bag, you **sortera** (sort) your **sopor** (rubbish) into several **kärl** (bins), often kept in a shared **miljörum** (recycling room) in the basement of an apartment building.

```example
Kan du slänga soporna? — Can you take out the rubbish?
Vi sorterar soporna i olika kärl. — We sort the rubbish into different bins.
```

Food scraps go in the bag for **komposterbart** (compostable waste), while everything else that can be recycled is split by material: **glas** (glass, often further split into coloured and clear), **metall** (metal, such as tin cans), **papper** (paper, like newspapers), and **plast** (plastic packaging). Before you sort a **förpackning** (packaging), it's good practice to **tömma** (empty) and **skölja** (rinse) it first.

```example
Skölj burken innan du sorterar den. — Rinse the jar before sorting it.
Släng inte batterier i restavfallet. — Don't throw batteries in the residual waste.
```

Things that don't fit any recycling category become **restavfall** (residual waste). Special items need special handling: **batteri** (batteries) and **farligt avfall** (hazardous waste, like paint or chemicals) go in dedicated collection boxes, and large items like old furniture — **grovsopor** (bulky waste) — must be taken to an **återvinningsstation** (recycling station), often reachable by car on weekends.

Learning these words makes it much easier to follow instructions posted in a stairwell or recycling room, and to sound **miljövänlig** (environmentally friendly) in daily conversation about the household.
