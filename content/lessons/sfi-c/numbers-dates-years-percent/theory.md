You already know numbers up to a thousand. Now put them to work in three
everyday situations: years, percentages, and simple fractions.

**Years** are said as one long compound number, built exactly like the big
numbers you already know — never digit by digit.

```example
nittonhundraåttiofyra — nineteen eighty-four (1984)
tjugohundratjugofem — twenty twenty-five (2025)
tjugotjugofem — twenty twenty-five (short, informal form of 2025)
```

Notice that years from 2000 onward have two common spoken forms: the full
**tjugohundra...** ("twenty-hundred...") and the shorter **tjugo...**
("twenty...") style, similar to how English speakers say both "two thousand
twenty-five" and "twenty twenty-five". Older years (1100–1999) almost always
use the **-hundra-** pattern, as in **nittonhundra** for 1900.

```example
Jag är född nittonhundraåttiofyra. — I was born in nineteen eighty-four.
Vilket årtal är det? Det är tjugotjugofem. — What year is it? It's twenty twenty-five.
```

**Percentages** are simple: cardinal number + **procent**.

```example
Femtio procent av eleverna är här. — Fifty percent of the students are here.
Priset ökade med tio procent. — The price increased by ten percent.
Jag är hundra procent säker. — I am one hundred percent sure.
```

For parts and multiples of a whole, Swedish has a small set of handy words.
**Hälften** ("half") is a noun and usually goes with **av** ("of"). **Dubbelt**
("double, twice as much") is an adverb describing an amount. Fractions like "a
third" or "a quarter" are built with **-del** ("part"): **en tredjedel**, **en
fjärdedel**.

```example
Hälften av kakan är kvar. — Half of the cake is left.
Det kostar dubbelt så mycket. — It costs twice as much.
En tredjedel av gruppen kom sent. — A third of the group arrived late.
```

Put together, these building blocks let you talk about growth, discounts, and
dates the way native speakers do — in the news, on receipts, and in everyday
conversation about "when" and "how much."
