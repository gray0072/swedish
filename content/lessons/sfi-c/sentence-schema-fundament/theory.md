You've learned two separate rules: the verb is always second (**V2**), and
sentence adverbs like **inte** sit in a fixed spot after the subject. Now
let's combine them into one tool: the **sentence schema** (*satsschema*) —
a row of slots that every Swedish main clause fills in the same order.

```example
Jag läser inte tidningen idag. — I'm not reading the newspaper today.
```

| fundament | verb | subject | adverb | rest of predicate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jag | läser | — | inte | tidningen idag |
| Idag | läser | jag | inte | tidningen |
| Tyvärr | kan | jag | inte | komma imorgon |

The **fundament** is simply whatever slot 1 holds — a subject, a time word,
or something else entirely. The **verb** always comes second, no exceptions.
If the subject is not already in the fundament, it moves to slot 3, right
after the verb. Slot 4 holds a **sentence adverb** such as *inte, tyvärr,
kanske* — it always comes after the subject, whether or not the subject was
the fundament. Everything left over — objects, places, infinitives — falls
into the last slot, the **rest of the predicate**.

```example
Idag läser jag inte tidningen. — Today I'm not reading the newspaper.
Tyvärr kan jag inte komma imorgon. — Unfortunately, I can't come tomorrow.
Kanske läser hon boken imorgon. — Maybe she'll read the book tomorrow.
```

Notice how the schema instantly explains word order that once needed two
separate rules: whatever starts the sentence goes in the fundament, the verb
must follow it directly, the subject slides to third place if it wasn't
first, and *inte/tyvärr/kanske* always land right after the subject — never
before the verb.

A practical trick: when building a sentence, fill the schema left to right.
Decide your fundament first, put the verb right after it, then the subject
(if needed), then any sentence adverb, and finally everything else.
