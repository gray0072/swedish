At the **apotek** (pharmacy) you don't always need a doctor's note — many medicines are **receptfria** (over the counter), meaning you can just ask for them at the counter. A common question to start with is **"Vad har ni mot huvudvärk?"** ("What do you have for a headache?")

```example
Vad har ni mot huvudvärk? — What do you have for a headache?
Jag behöver några värktabletter. — I need some painkillers.
```

If you're **förkyld** (have a cold), the pharmacist might suggest a **nässpray** (nasal spray) for a stuffy nose, **halstabletter** (throat lozenges) for a sore throat, or something **febernedsättande** (fever-reducing) if you also have a **förkylning** with fever. If you're **hostar** (coughing) a lot, mention that too.

```example
Jag är förkyld och har ont i halsen. — I have a cold and a sore throat.
Har ni något febernedsättande? — Do you have anything fever-reducing?
```

Always check the **dosering** (dosage) on the package — how many **doser** (doses) to take and how often — and ask the **farmaceut** (pharmacist) if you're unsure. It's also fine to ask, **"Finns det några biverkningar?"** ("Are there any side effects?") For small cuts, don't forget a **plåster** (band-aid).

With this small set of words, a trip to the pharmacy for everyday complaints — headache, cold, cough, sore throat, or a small wound — becomes straightforward, even without a prescription.
