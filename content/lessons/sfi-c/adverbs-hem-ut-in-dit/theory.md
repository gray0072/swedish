Swedish has a family of place adverbs that come in **pairs**: one form for
*movement toward* a place, and a different form for *being located* there.
This is the same movement-vs-location idea you saw with prepositions, but
now built into single adverbs instead of preposition phrases.

```example
Jag går hem klockan fem. — I go home at five. (movement)
Jag är hemma ikväll. — I am at home tonight. (location)
```

**Hem** vs **hemma** works exactly like i vs på vs till: hem answers "where
to?", hemma answers "where?". The same pattern repeats with **ut/ute** (out
vs outside) and **in/inne** (in vs inside):

```example
Han går ut i trädgården. — He goes out into the garden. (movement)
Barnen leker ute. — The children are playing outside. (location)
Kom in! — Come in! (movement)
Vi är inne nu, det regnar. — We are inside now, it's raining. (location)
```

A fourth pair, **dit/där**, works the same way but points at a place already
mentioned or understood, rather than "home," "out," or "in":

```example
Jag ska dit imorgon. — I'm going there tomorrow. (movement)
Min syster bor där. — My sister lives there. (location)
```

A simple memory trick: the "location" forms — **hemma, ute, inne, där** — all
end in **-e** or **-a**, and answer the question *var?* ("where?"). The
"movement" forms — **hem, ut, in, dit** — are shorter, and answer *vart?*
("where to?").

```example
Var är du? – Hemma. / Ute. / Inne. / Där.
Vart ska du? – Hem. / Ut. / In. / Dit.
```

Mixing these up is one of the most common mistakes for learners — but once
you tie each word to *var* or *vart*, the right choice becomes automatic.
