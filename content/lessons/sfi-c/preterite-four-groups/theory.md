In kurs B you memorised a handful of preterite (past-tense) forms as ready-made
words. Now it is time to see the system behind them. Swedish verbs fall into
four groups, and each group forms the preterite in its own predictable way.

**Group 1** — the largest group, verbs ending in **-ar** in the present —
add **-ade**.

```example
Han arbetar. – Han arbetade. — He worked.
Vi talar om jobbet. – Vi talade om jobbet. — We talked about work.
```

**Group 2** — verbs whose stem ends in a consonant — add **-de** (after a
voiced consonant) or **-te** (after an unvoiced one like s, k, p).

```example
Jag ringer. – Jag ringde. — I called.
Hon läser en bok. – Hon läste en bok. — She read a book.
```

**Group 3** — short verbs ending in a stressed vowel (bo, tro, ske) — add
**-dde**.

```example
Vi bor i Malmö. – Vi bodde i Malmö. — We lived in Malmö.
Jag tror på dig. – Jag trodde på dig. — I believed you.
```

**Group 4** — the strong, irregular verbs — take no ending at all. Instead
the stem vowel changes.

```example
Hon skriver ett brev. – Hon skrev ett brev. — She wrote a letter.
Vi äter middag. – Vi åt middag. — We ate dinner.
```

Here is the whole system side by side:

| group | pattern | example 1 | example 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -ar → -ade | arbeta → arbetade | tala → talade |
| 2 | consonant stem → -de/-te | ringa → ringde | läsa → läste |
| 3 | stressed vowel → -dde | bo → bodde | tro → trodde |
| 4 | vowel change, no ending | skriva → skrev | äta → åt |

There is no shortcut for group 4 — each strong verb's preterite has to be
learned individually, the way you already learned var, gick and kom. But
groups 1–3 are fully predictable once you know which group a verb belongs
to, which is usually visible from its present-tense ending.
