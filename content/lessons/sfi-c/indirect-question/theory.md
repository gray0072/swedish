A direct question and a question embedded inside a polite request look
different in Swedish, even though they ask the same thing. Compare:

```example
Var är stationen? — Where is the station? (direct question)
Vet du var stationen är? — Do you know where the station is? (embedded)
```

In the direct question the verb **är** comes right after **var**. Once you
embed the question inside "Vet du...", the word order changes to match a
normal subordinate clause: the subject comes right after the question word,
and the verb follows it — **var stationen är**, not *var är stationen*.

```example
Vet du var jag kan parkera? — Do you know where I can park?
```

When there is no question word — a plain yes/no question — Swedish uses the
conjunction **om** ("if/whether") to introduce the embedded question, again
with subordinate word order:

```example
Kan du säga om affären är öppen? — Can you say if the shop is open?
Jag undrar om hon kommer. — I wonder if she is coming.
```

This pattern is common after verbs like **veta** (to know), **undra** (to
wonder), and **fråga** (to ask), and it makes a request sound noticeably more
polite than a bare question.

```example
Veta: Jag vet inte var han bor. — I don't know where he lives.
Undra: Jag undrar om hon kommer. — I wonder if she's coming.
Fråga: Han frågade om jag visste var stationen var. — He asked if I knew
where the station was.
```

A short checklist: find the question word (or use **om** if there isn't
one), then put the subject first and the verb after it — just like any other
subordinate clause.
