When you feel unwell in Sweden, the first stop is usually the **vårdcentral** (local health center), not the **sjukhus** (hospital) — that's for emergencies. You call or visit to **boka en tid** (book an appointment) with a **läkare** (doctor) or **sjuksköterska** (nurse).

```example
Jag mår inte bra. — I don't feel well.
Jag har ont i huvudet. — I have a headache.
```

Swedish often describes symptoms with the pattern **ha ont i + body part**: *ont i magen* (stomach ache), *ont i halsen* (sore throat). **Feber** means fever, and if you're coughing a lot you say **jag hostar**. A doctor's note that lists your symptoms is called a **symtom**-lista in everyday speech, though the word itself, **symtom**, just means "symptom."

```example
Jag har feber och hostar. — I have a fever and I'm coughing.
Var ligger närmaste apotek? — Where's the nearest pharmacy?
```

After the visit, the doctor may write a **recept** (prescription), which you take to the **apotek** (pharmacy) to pick up your **medicin**. If the pain is more general, Swedes also use the noun **smärta** — a slightly more formal word for pain than the everyday phrase "ha ont."

Remember the opposite pair: **sjuk** means sick, and **frisk** means healthy — *Jag var sjuk i fredags, men nu är jag frisk igen* ("I was sick on Friday, but now I'm healthy again"). Knowing this handful of words is usually enough to explain what's wrong and get the right kind of help.
