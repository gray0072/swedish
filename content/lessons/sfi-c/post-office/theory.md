In Sweden, you rarely visit a dedicated post office building — instead, most towns have a **postombud** (postal agent), a counter inside a grocery store or kiosk that handles mail. This is where you go to **skicka** (send) a **paket** (parcel) or **hämta ut ett paket** (pick up a parcel).

```example
Jag ska hämta ut ett paket på postombudet. — I need to pick up a parcel at the post office agent.
Kan jag skicka det här paketet till Ryssland? — Can I send this parcel to Russia?
```

A simple **brev** (letter) needs a **frimärke** (stamp) and goes into a **kuvert** (envelope) with the **mottagare** (recipient) written on the front and the **avsändare** (sender) usually on the back. Once stamped, drop it in a yellow **postlåda** (postbox) on the street.

```example
Jag behöver ett frimärke till det här brevet. — I need a stamp for this letter.
Lägg brevet i den gula postlådan. — Put the letter in the yellow postbox.
```

For parcels, the **porto** (postage) depends on the **vikt** (weight) and destination. Sending abroad, you'll usually get a **spårningsnummer** (tracking number) so you can follow where the parcel is. When a parcel arrives for you, you don't always get it directly at home — instead, a slip called an **avi** (delivery notice) appears in your mailbox, telling you to collect it at the postombud. Bring **legitimation** (ID) — the **mottagare** must prove who they are before the parcel is handed over.

```example
Portot beror på paketets vikt. — The postage depends on the parcel's weight.
Ta med legitimation när du hämtar paketet. — Bring ID when you pick up the parcel.
```

With this handful of words, you can send letters, mail parcels, and collect deliveries with confidence.
