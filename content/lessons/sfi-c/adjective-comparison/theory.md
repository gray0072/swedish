Swedish adjectives compare in three degrees, just like English "big, bigger,
biggest": the **positive** (base form), the **comparative** (more of a
quality), and the **superlative** (most of a quality).

Most adjectives form these regularly with **-are** and **-ast**:

```example
stor – större – störst — big – bigger – biggest
dyr – dyrare – dyrast — expensive – more expensive – most expensive
lång – längre – längst — long/tall – longer/taller – longest/tallest
```

Use **än** for "than" in comparisons:

```example
Bilen är dyrare än huset. — The car is more expensive than the house.
```

A handful of very common adjectives are irregular and must be memorized —
just like English "good, better, best" doesn't follow "gooder, goodest":

| positive | comparative | superlative | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| bra | bättre | bäst | good |
| dålig | sämre | sämst | bad |
| liten | mindre | minst | small |
| gammal | äldre | äldst | old |
| många | fler | flest | many |

```example
Det här är bättre än det gamla. — This is better than the old one.
Min bror är äldre än jag. — My brother is older than me.
Hon har fler böcker än jag. — She has more books than me.
```

The comparative form (bättre, större, äldre...) never changes for gender or
number — one comparative form fits en-words, ett-words, and plurals alike.
The superlative, however, usually takes an ending when used with a definite
noun (den störst**a** bilen, den bäst**a** boken) — a detail explored further
alongside the definite adjective. For now, focus on recognizing and producing
the three degrees correctly, especially the five irregular adjectives above,
since they appear extremely often in everyday Swedish.
