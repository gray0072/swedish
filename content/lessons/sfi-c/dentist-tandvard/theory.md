When a tooth starts hurting, you don't wait — you call a **tandläkare** (dentist) directly, using the same phrasing you already know for appointments: **Jag vill boka tid hos tandläkaren** (I'd like to book a dentist appointment). Describing the problem is simple: **Jag har tandvärk** (I have a toothache), or more specifically, **Jag har ont i en tand** (I have pain in one tooth).

```example
Jag vill boka tid hos tandläkaren. — I'd like to book a dentist appointment.
Jag har fruktansvärd tandvärk. — I have terrible toothache.
```

At the clinic, a **tandsköterska** (dental assistant) usually helps first, then the dentist examines your **tänder** (teeth), often checking for **karies** (cavities) with an X-ray. If a cavity is found, you'll need a **lagning** (filling) — the dentist may ask **Vill du ha bedövning?** (Would you like an anesthetic?) before starting.

```example
Du behöver en lagning i den här tanden. — You need a filling in this tooth.
Vill du ha bedövning innan lagningen? — Would you like an anesthetic before the filling?
```

Once the **behandling** (treatment) is done, the clinic sends a **räkning** (bill) — Swedish dental care, unlike medical care, isn't free for adults, so the **tandvårdskostnad** (cost of dental care) can add up. A subsidy called **frisktandvård** makes regular **kontroller** (check-ups) cheaper if you sign up for it, so it's worth asking about at your first visit.

```example
Tandvårdskostnaden var högre än jag trodde. — The cost of the dental care was higher than I expected.
Det är dags för din årliga kontroll. — It's time for your annual check-up.
```

With these phrases, you can book a visit, explain what hurts, and understand the bill that follows.
