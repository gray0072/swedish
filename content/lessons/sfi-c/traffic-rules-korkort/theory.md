Getting a Swedish **körkort** (driving licence) follows a clear path, and it
comes with its own vocabulary.

```example
Jag vill ta körkort nästa år. — I want to get a driving licence next year.
```

First comes a **körkortstillstånd** (learner's permit), which lets you start
**övningsköra** (practising driving) — often with family, or with a
professional **körlärare** at a **trafikskola** (driving school).

```example
Man måste ha körkortstillstånd innan man övningskör. — You need a learner's permit before practising to drive.
Min körlärare är väldigt tålmodig. — My driving instructor is very patient.
```

Two exams stand between you and the licence: the **teoriprov** (written
theory test) and the **uppkörning** (practical driving test).

```example
Jag har bokat teoriprov på fredag. — I've booked the theory test on Friday.
Uppkörningen tar cirka en timme. — The driving test takes about an hour.
```

Behind the wheel you need to know the basics of **trafikregler** (traffic
rules): stop at a **trafikljus** (traffic light), read a **vägmärke** (road
sign), keep to your **körfält** (lane), watch the **hastighetsbegränsning**
(speed limit) — sometimes enforced by a **fartkamera** (speed camera) — and
always wear your **bilbälte** (seatbelt).

```example
Det är viktigt att följa trafikreglerna. — It's important to follow the traffic rules.
Stanna vid rött trafikljus. — Stop at a red traffic light.
Håll dig i höger körfält. — Keep to the right-hand lane.
Kom ihåg att ta på dig bilbältet. — Remember to put on your seatbelt.
```

If things go wrong despite all that, the word you hope never to need is
**trafikolycka** — a traffic accident.

```example
Det var en trafikolycka på motorvägen. — There was a traffic accident on the motorway.
```
