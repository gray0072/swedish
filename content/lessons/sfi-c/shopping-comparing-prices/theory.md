Before buying something bigger, many Swedes like to **jämföra priser** (compare prices) between shops or online. Look out for a **rabatt** (discount) or a whole **rea** (sale), often marked with a red sign in the window.

```example
Jag brukar jämföra priser innan jag köper något dyrt. — I usually compare prices before buying something expensive.
Butiken har rea på skor just nu. — The shop has a sale on shoes right now.
```

If something turns out to be **trasig** (broken) or simply the wrong size, you can go back to the shop. To **lämna tillbaka** a purchase means to return it; to **byta** it means to exchange it for another size or colour. Many shops in Sweden also offer **öppet köp** — the right to return an unused item within a set number of days, no reason needed, as long as you have the **kvitto** (receipt).

```example
Kan jag lämna tillbaka den här tröjan? — Can I return this sweater?
Jag vill byta den mot en större storlek. — I'd like to exchange it for a bigger size.
```

If the item is actually faulty, the word you need is **reklamera** — to make a formal complaint about a purchase, usually asking for a repair, exchange, or **pengarna tillbaka** (a refund). Many products also come with a **garanti** (warranty) that covers this.

```example
Jag vill reklamera den här produkten, den är trasig. — I want to complain about this product, it's broken.
Jag vill ha pengarna tillbaka. — I would like a refund.
```

Knowing the difference between **dyr** (expensive), **billig** (cheap) and **prisvärd** (good value) helps you talk about whether a deal is actually worth it, not just cheap.
