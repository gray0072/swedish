Two different adverb topics share this lesson: how to turn an adjective into
an adverb, and a small set of place adverbs that come in movement/location
pairs.

## From adjective to adverb: add -t

Most Swedish adjectives form their adverb the same way they form their
neuter (ett-word) form — by adding **-t**.

```example
Han sprang snabbt. — He ran fast. (snabb → snabbt)
Hon pratade lugnt. — She spoke calmly. (lugn → lugnt)
```

If you already know an adjective's ett-form, you already know its adverb —
they're identical in spelling.

## Movement versus location: a family of pairs

A handful of very common place words come in two forms: one for *movement
toward* a place, one for simply *being* there.

| movement | location | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| hem | hemma | home(ward) / at home |
| ut | ute | out(ward) / outside |
| in | inne | in(ward) / inside |
| upp | uppe | up(ward) / up there |
| ner | nere | down(ward) / down there |
| dit | där | (to) there / there |
| hit | här | (to) here / here |

```example
Jag går hem. Jag är hemma. — I'm going home. I'm at home.
Hon går ut. Hon är ute. — She's going out. She's outside.
Vi ska åka dit. Vi bor där. — We're going there. We live there.
Kom hit! Jag är här. — Come here! I am here.
```

## Choosing the right one

The test is simple: does the verb describe going somewhere, or being
somewhere? A verb of movement (**gå, åka, komma, klättra**) takes the
movement form; a verb of state (**vara, bo, sitta, stå**) takes the
location form.

```example
Katten klättrar upp. Katten är uppe. — The cat climbs up. The cat is up (there).
```

## In short

Add **-t** to most adjectives to get their adverb, and pick the movement
form of a place word for verbs of going, the location form for verbs of
being.
