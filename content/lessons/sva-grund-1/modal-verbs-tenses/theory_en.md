Modal verbs (vilja, kunna, måste, få, böra, ska) don't follow the regular conjugation
groups — their forms simply have to be learned. The supine is what lets a modal verb enter
the perfect: **har + supine + the infinitive of the main verb**.

| Verb | Present | Preteritum | Supine | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **vilja** (to want) | vill | ville | velat | Jag har alltid velat lära mig svenska. |
| **kunna** (can, to be able to) | kan | kunde | kunnat | Hon har aldrig kunnat simma. |
| **få** (to be allowed to, may) | får | fick | fått | Vi har äntligen fått resa till Sverige. |
| **böra** (ought to) | bör | borde | bort | Han borde ha ringt tidigare. |
| **måste** (must, have to) | måste | måste | måst | Jag har måst jobba hela helgen. |
| **ska** (going to, the "future") | ska | skulle | — | Hon skulle resa, men blev sjuk. |

## Three quirks worth noting

**måste** has the exact same form in the present and the preteritum — the tense only shows
through context: *Jag måste jobba nu* (now) vs. *Igår måste jag jobba* (yesterday).

The supine of **böra** (*bort*) sounds formal and is rare in speech — more often the
preteritum **borde** is used on its own, without *har*, as a gentle piece of advice: *Du
borde vila* ("You should rest").

**ska** is almost never used in the perfect — instead, the preteritum **skulle** covers
the sense of "was going to": *Hon skulle resa, men blev sjuk* — "She was going to travel,
but fell ill."

```example
Jag vill lära mig svenska. — I want to learn Swedish.
Jag har alltid velat lära mig svenska. — I've always wanted to learn Swedish.
Du får inte parkera här. — You're not allowed to park here.
Vi har äntligen fått resa till Sverige. — We finally got to travel to Sweden.
```

After a modal verb, the next verb is always a bare infinitive, with no **att**: *vill
simma*, *kan simma*, *måste simma* — more on that in "att + the infinitive".
