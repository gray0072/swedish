Sentence adverbs (*satsadverbial*) are words like **inte, alltid, aldrig, ofta, kanske,
redan, snart**. They comment on the whole statement — negation, frequency, likelihood,
time — rather than describing a single action, and their placement follows one shared
rule.

| Satsadverbial | Translation | Position in huvudsats | Position in bisats |
|---|---|---|---|
| inte | not | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| alltid | always | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| aldrig | never | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| ofta | often | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| kanske | maybe | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| redan | already | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |
| snart | soon | after the finite verb | before the finite verb |

This generalises the **BIFF** rule from the previous lesson: it's not just "inte" — the
whole family of sentence adverbs behaves the same way.

```example
Hon kommer alltid i tid. — She always arrives on time.
Jag vet att hon alltid kommer i tid. — I know that she always arrives on time.
```

Notice: "alltid" comes after "kommer" in the huvudsats, but before "kommer" in the
bisats after "att" — the subjekt "hon" stays put in both, only the adverb moves.

```example
De ringer aldrig på kvällen. — They never call in the evening.
Det är konstigt att de aldrig ringer på kvällen. — It's odd that they never call in the evening.
```

One more contrast, with "redan":

```example
Barnen sover redan. — The children are already asleep.
Jag hoppas att barnen redan sover. — I hope the children are already asleep.
```

A practical tip: whenever you're unsure where to put "alltid/ofta/kanske" — ask
yourself whether this is a huvudsats or a bisats (is there a conjunction like
*att/eftersom/om/när* in front of it?), and apply the same rule you already know for
"inte".
