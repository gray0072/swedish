Retelling a story you've read isn't about memory — it's about the right **connectors**
(*sambandsord*) that show the order of events: **först** ("first"), **sedan** and
**därefter** ("then", "after that"), **plötsligt** ("suddenly") and **till slut**
("finally", "in the end").

```example
Först gick Anna till affären. — First Anna went to the store.
Sedan tappade hon sin telefon på gatan. — Then she dropped her phone on the street.
Plötsligt ropade någon: "Du tappade något!" — Suddenly someone shouted: "You dropped something!"
Till slut fick hon tillbaka telefonen av en snäll man. — In the end a kind man gave her the phone back.
```

The second key tool is reporting verbs: **berätta att** ("to tell that"), **säga att**
("to say that"), **tycka att** ("to think/feel that"). What follows is an **att-sats** —
a subordinate clause that obeys the same BIFF rule you already learned: if "inte" sits
after the verb in the main clause, it jumps to **before** the verb inside the att-clause.

```example
Hon sa att hon inte kunde komma. — She said she couldn't come.
Han berättade att han hade glömt nyckeln. — He told me he had forgotten the key.
Jag tycker att historien var rolig. — I think the story was funny.
```

Compare: **Hon kunde inte komma** (huvudsats, "inte" after the verb) → **Hon sa att hon
inte kunde komma** (bisats after att, "inte" before the verb).

To keep a retelling coherent, stick to one **handling** ("plot, storyline"), call the
**huvudperson** ("main character") by name, and use **börja** and **sluta** — "to begin"
and "to end": "Berättelsen börjar på morgonen och slutar på kvällen" ("The story begins
in the morning and ends in the evening").
