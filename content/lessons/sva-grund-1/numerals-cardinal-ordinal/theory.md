You already know numbers for counting and prices. Now systematise them into
**grundtal** (cardinals: one, two, three...) and **ordningstal** (ordinals:
first, second, third...), and use both to read dates and clock times aloud.

## Cardinals to ordinals

The first three ordinals are irregular and must be memorised; from four
onward, the pattern is mostly regular: cardinal + **-e** (with small stem
changes).

```example
ett, två, tre → första, andra, tredje — one, two, three → first, second,
third
fyra → fjärde — four → fourth
tio → tionde — ten → tenth
```

## Dates: ordinal + month

A date is read as **den** + ordinal + month name, all lowercase.

```example
Idag är det den tredje maj. — Today is the third of May.
Han föddes den första januari. — He was born on the first of January.
```

## Clock time: halv, kvart

Swedish clock time counts *toward* the next hour, not from the last one —
this trips up many learners, so pay close attention to **halv**.

```example
Klockan är halv åtta. — It's half past seven. (halfway TO eight, i.e. 7:30,
not 8:30!)
Klockan är kvart i nio. — It's a quarter to nine. (8:45)
Klockan är kvart över nio. — It's a quarter past nine. (9:15)
```

## Years: read as two two-digit chunks (until 2000)

Years from 1100 to 1999 are read as two joined two-digit numbers, with no
pause between them; years from 2000 on are usually read as one long number
or, informally, split the same old way.

```example
1984 → nittonhundraåttiofyra — nineteen eighty-four
2025 → tjugohundratjugofem (formal) or tjugotjugofem (casual) — twenty
twenty-five
```

## In short

Learn *första/andra/tredje* by heart, build the rest with **-e**; read a
date as **den + ordinal + month**; and remember Swedish clock time looks
forward to the next hour, not back to the last one.
