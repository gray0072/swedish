**Konsumenträtt** (consumer rights) protects the **konsument** (consumer) in dealings
with a **näringsidkare** (trader). If a purchased item turns out to be faulty, the buyer
has **reklamationsrätt** — the right to make a complaint, which in Sweden applies for up
to three years. Within the first two years after purchase, the **bevisbörda** (burden of
proving a defect is not the seller's fault) usually lies with the seller.

```example
Konsumenträtten skyddar dig som köper varor och tjänster. — Consumer law protects you as someone who buys goods and services.
Reklamationsrätten gäller i upp till tre år. — The right to make a complaint applies for up to three years.
```

Do not confuse **reklamationsrätt** with **öppet köp** (an open-purchase return right) —
the latter is not guaranteed by law and depends on the individual store's own policy. A
separate rule is the **ångerrätt**, the right to cancel a purchase made online or by
phone, usually within 14 days. To **reklamera** (make a complaint), it helps to keep the
**kvitto** (receipt) as proof of purchase.

```example
Butiken erbjuder öppet köp i 30 dagar. — The store offers an open-purchase return policy for 30 days.
Ångerrätten gäller i 14 dagar vid köp på nätet. — The right of withdrawal applies for 14 days for online purchases.
```

If a **tvist** (dispute) arises between the consumer and the trader and they cannot
agree directly, one can **anmäla ett ärende** (file a case) free of charge with **ARN**
(Allmänna reklamationsnämnden) — the government board that reviews consumer disputes
without going to court.

```example
Om butiken inte svarar kan du anmäla ärendet till ARN. — If the store does not respond, you can take the case to ARN.
ARN erbjuder en enkel form av tvistlösning utan domstol. — ARN offers a simple form of dispute resolution without going to court.
```

It is important to understand that ARN's decision is a **rekommendation**
(recommendation), not a binding court ruling — though most companies follow it
voluntarily.
