Two constructions let you add extra information inside a sentence without
starting a new one — an **apposition** and a **parenthetical insertion**.

## Apposition: a second name for the same thing

An apposition is a noun phrase placed right next to another, naming or
describing the same person or thing a second time. It's set off by commas on
both sides (or ends the sentence, with one comma).

```example
Anna, min syster, kommer imorgon. — Anna, my sister, is coming tomorrow.
Stockholm, Sveriges huvudstad, ligger vid vattnet. — Stockholm, Sweden's
capital, is by the water.
Ingvar Kamprad, grundaren av IKEA, föddes i Småland. — Ingvar Kamprad, the
founder of IKEA, was born in Småland.
```

Both parts of an apposition refer to the *same* entity — "Anna" and "min
syster" are one and the same person, just named twice.

## Parenthetical insertion: a comment slipped in

A parenthetical insertion is a word, adverb or short phrase — often a
personal comment, a reservation, or an evaluation — dropped into the middle
of a sentence and set off by commas (or, for a stronger break, dashes).

```example
Det var, tyvärr, för sent för att ändra planerna. — It was, unfortunately,
too late to change the plans.
Det är, enligt min mening, det bästa alternativet. — It is, in my opinion,
the best option.
Priset steg — för övrigt inte för första gången — med tio procent. — The
price rose — incidentally not for the first time — by ten percent.
```

Unlike an apposition, a parenthetical insertion doesn't rename anything — it
comments on the sentence from the side, and could be lifted out entirely
without changing the sentence's basic meaning.

## In short

Both constructions sit inside the sentence, marked off by commas: an
apposition adds a second label for the same thing, a parenthetical insertion
adds the speaker's own remark. Read the sentence aloud without the inserted
part — it should still stand on its own.
