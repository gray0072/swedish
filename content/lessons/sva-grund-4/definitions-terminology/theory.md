An utredande text often introduces a term the reader may not know. Swedish has a
small set of fixed patterns for exactly this move: presenting and defining a
concept.

## Four ways to define a concept

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| X är + definition | En allemansrätt är rätten att vistas fritt i naturen. |
| X innebär att... | Källkritik innebär att man bedömer en källas tillförlitlighet. |
| Med X menas/avses... | Med hållbar utveckling menas en utveckling som tillgodoser dagens behov utan att äventyra kommande generationers. |
| X kan definieras som... | Ett argument kan definieras som ett påstående som stöds av skäl. |

```example
En allemansrätt är rätten att vistas fritt i naturen. — The right of public access is the right to move about freely in nature.
Källkritik innebär att man bedömer en källas tillförlitlighet. — Source criticism means assessing the reliability of a source.
```

`Med X menas...` (and its more formal twin `med X avses...`) is passive in feel:
it puts the concept first and backgrounds the person doing the defining —
suited to careful, official writing.

## From everyday sense to precise sense

A common move is to give the word's ordinary meaning first, then narrow it to
the sense used in this particular text:

```example
I vardagligt tal betyder ordet "kultur" konst och nöje, men i den här texten avses människors gemensamma levnadssätt. — In everyday speech the word "culture" means art and entertainment, but in this text it refers to a shared way of life.
```

## Marking the term

A term that is being defined is usually set apart on its first mention — in
**bold**, in *italics*, or in "quotation marks" — so the reader can spot it and
find it again later in the text. After that first, marked mention, the term is
simply used like any other word.

```example
En **term** som definieras bör markeras första gången den används. — A term that is being defined should be marked the first time it's used.
```

## In short

`X är...`, `X innebär att...`, `Med X menas/avses...` and `X kan definieras
som...` are the standard frames for introducing a concept in Swedish; marking
the term on first mention helps the reader track it. This closes the SVA grund
programme — 200 grammar points end here, with the tool for naming precisely what
you are talking about.
