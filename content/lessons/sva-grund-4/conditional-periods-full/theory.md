This lesson is a final summary of all the conditional periods (villkorssatser)
seen at earlier levels: from the simple real condition to the unreal past
condition and the formal inversion without `om`.

## Summary table

| Type | Subordinate clause (om-part) | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real (future/general fact) | present | present | Om jag har tid, ringer jag dig. |
| Unreal (present) | past (preterite) | skulle + infinitive | Om jag hade tid, skulle jag ringa dig. |
| Unreal (past) | pluperfect | skulle ha + supine | Om jag hade haft tid, skulle jag ha ringt dig. |
| No om (inversion) | verb-subject inversion | skulle (ha) + infinitive/supine | Hade jag haft tid, skulle jag ha ringt dig. |

```example
Om jag har tid ringer jag dig. — If I have time, I'll call you. (real)
Om jag hade tid skulle jag ringa dig. — If I had time, I would call you. (unreal, present)
Om jag hade haft tid skulle jag ha ringt dig. — If I had had time (then), I would have called you. (unreal, past)
Hade jag haft tid, skulle jag ha ringt dig. — Had I had time, I would have called you. (no om)
```

## How to tell the types apart

1. **Real condition** — both halves in the present; refers to the future or a
   general rule, quite achievable.
2. **Unreal, present** — subordinate clause in the past tense, main clause with
   `skulle` + infinitive; the condition isn't met right now.
3. **Unreal, past** — subordinate clause in the pluperfect (`hade` + supine),
   main clause with `skulle ha` + supine; the condition wasn't met back then, and
   nothing can change that now.
4. **No om** — `om` is dropped and inversion takes over its role, moving the
   finite verb to first position. This works for any of the three types above,
   and for real conditions too — a stylistic, more formal variant, not a fourth
   meaning.

## One more pair to compare

```example
Uppstår fel, kontakta support. — If an error occurs, contact support. (real, no om)
Vore jag dig, skulle jag tacka nej. — Were I you, I would decline. (unreal present, archaic vore, no om)
```

## In short

The types differ in the tense of the subordinate clause (present / past /
pluperfect) and the form of the main clause (present / skulle + infinitive /
skulle ha + supine). Dropping `om` and fronting the verb works for any type — a
matter of style, not of meaning.
