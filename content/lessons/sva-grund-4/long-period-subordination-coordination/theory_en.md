Delkurs 3 showed how to build complex sentences with subordinate clauses (bisatser).
Now comes the stylistic question: in a long paragraph you can chain ideas through
**coordination** (samordning: `och`, `men`, `så`) or embed them inside each other
through **subordination** (underordning: `eftersom`, `som`, `vilket`, `trots att`).
The content stays the same — the effect changes.

## Coordination vs subordination

| | Coordination (samordning) | Subordination (underordning) |
|---|---|---|
| The parts | equal in status | one main clause (huvudsats), the rest subordinate (bisatser) |
| Effect | a list of facts, additive | hierarchy: one idea backgrounded, another foregrounded |
| Register | plainer, closer to speech | denser, written, typical of academic style |

## Same content, two versions

```example
Det regnade, vi hade lovat att komma, så vi gick ut ändå, och vi blev blöta. — It was raining, we had promised to come, so we went out anyway, and we got wet.
Trots att det regnade gick vi ut, eftersom vi hade lovat att komma, vilket gjorde att vi blev blöta. — Although it was raining, we went out, because we had promised to come, which meant that we got wet.
```

In the first version, four facts stand in a row as equals — a typical coordinated
chain. In the second, the same information is restructured: the main clause becomes
*vi gick ut* ("we went out") — the other three ideas are subordinated around it
(`trots att` — concession, `eftersom` — reason, `vilket` — consequence). The reader
immediately sees which idea is central and which is background.

## The risk of an overlong period

Subordination makes a text denser, but not without limit: too many embedded bisatser
in one sentence tires the reader and hurts readability. A skilled writer doesn't
subordinate everything at once, but **varies sentence length** — alternating short,
coordinated phrases with dense, subordinated periods.

## In short

Coordination and subordination can express the same content in different ways:
coordination lays out a row of equal facts, subordination builds a hierarchy where
one idea comes to the foreground and the rest become background. The choice between
them is a stylistic decision by the writer, not a grammatical necessity.
