A complete argumentative essay follows a clear **disposition** (structure). It all begins with an introduction featuring a **krok** — a striking opening sentence that grabs attention — and a clearly stated **tes** (thesis), the main claim of the whole text.

```example
En stark krok fångar läsarens uppmärksamhet direkt i första meningen. — A strong hook grabs the reader's attention right in the first sentence.
Tesen bör presenteras tydligt redan i inledningen. — The thesis should be stated clearly already in the introduction.
```

Next come two or three body paragraphs. Each opens with an **ämnesmening** (topic sentence) — its own small point for that paragraph — followed by **belägg** (evidence: a fact, a quote, an example) and **analys** (analysis — explaining why that evidence supports the thesis).

```example
Ge alltid ett konkret belägg som stöder din åsikt, och analysera sedan varför det är relevant. — Always give concrete evidence that supports your point, then analyse why it is relevant.
```

A strong essay does not ignore the other side: one paragraph is devoted to a **motargument** (counter-argument), which must then be **bemöta** — properly countered with a **bemötande** (rebuttal), rather than simply brushed aside.

```example
Man skulle kunna invända att..., men jag bemöter det argumentet genom att... — One could object that..., but I counter that argument by...
```

The conclusion is not just a restatement of the thesis in other words. A good avslutning goes further: **mot bakgrund av detta** (in light of this) it can draw a broader conclusion or pose a new question to the reader.

```example
Sammanfattningsvis, och mot bakgrund av detta, öppnar frågan för en viktigare diskussion. — In conclusion, and in light of this, the question opens up a more important discussion.
```

A single **röd tråd** (common thread) should run through the entire text, connecting the introduction, every paragraph, and the conclusion.
