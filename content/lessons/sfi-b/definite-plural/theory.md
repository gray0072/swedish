You already know the five plural declensions (-or, -ar, -er, -n, no ending). Now
add one more layer: the **definite plural** ("the cars", not just "cars"). The rule
builds directly on the indefinite plural you already have — you just add a small
ending on top of it.

| declension | indefinite plural | + ending | definite plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| -or | flickor | **-na** | flickorna |
| -ar | bilar | **-na** | bilarna |
| -er | lägenheter | **-na** | lägenheterna |
| -n | äpplen | **-a** | äpplena |
| no ending | hus | **-en** | husen |

```example
flickor – flickorna — girls – the girls
bilar – bilarna — cars – the cars
lägenheter – lägenheterna — apartments – the apartments
äpplen – äpplena — apples – the apples
hus – husen — houses – the houses
```

The pattern in short: most plurals already ending in a vowel sound (-or, -ar, -er)
simply add **-na**. The **-n** declension, which already ends in "-n", just adds
**-a** (äpplen -> äpplena) rather than repeating the ending. And plurals with **no
ending** at all in the indefinite (ending in a consonant, like "hus" or "barn") add
**-en**.

```example
hundar – hundarna — dogs – the dogs
barn – barnen — children – the children
bord – borden — tables – the tables
```

So the whole singular-plural system now has four forms for every noun: indefinite
singular, definite singular, indefinite plural, definite plural. Once you know
which of the five declensions a noun belongs to, all four forms follow the same
predictable pattern — the only thing to memorise is the declension itself.
