Showing possession in Swedish is refreshingly simple: just add **-s** directly
to the end of the noun. No apostrophe, no separate word.

```example
Anna → Annas bok — Anna's book
mamma → mammas bil — mom's car
Erik → Eriks telefon — Erik's phone
```

This works with names, exactly like the examples above. It also works with
ordinary common nouns, and even with nouns that are already in the definite
form (the "the"-form). Just tack **-s** onto whatever ending is already there:

```example
barnet + s → barnets namn — the child's name
läraren + s → lärarens bok — the teacher's book
systern + s → systerns lägenhet — the sister's apartment
```

Notice **barnets**: the noun is already definite (**barnet**, "the child"),
and the genitive **-s** simply attaches after that, giving **barnets**. The
same pattern works for an indefinite noun too — you can say "ett barns
leksak" ("a child's toy") if the child isn't specific.

You can combine the genitive with a possessive pronoun, stacking two markers
of possession:

```example
min mammas bil — my mom's car
din väns namn — your friend's name
```

Here **mammas** already means "mom's", and **min** in front just adds "my"
to the whole phrase — "my mom's car", not "my's mom's car". Only the last
noun in the chain gets the **-s**.

One more thing to remember: Swedish never uses an apostrophe before the -s,
unlike English. Words that already end in **-s**, **-x** or **-z** (rare,
often foreign names) sometimes just add an apostrophe instead, but for
virtually every noun you'll use day to day, plain **-s** is all you need.
