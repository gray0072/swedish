Household chores in Swedish are mostly simple verbs you'll use with **måste** ("must")
or **ska** ("going to"), or in the imperative when telling someone what to do. The most
general word is **städa** — "to clean" the whole home — while specific chores have
their own verbs: **diska** (wash the dishes), **dammsuga** (vacuum), **tvätta** (do the
laundry), and **stryka** (iron).

```example
Jag städar lägenheten på lördagar. — I clean my apartment on Saturdays.
Kan du diska efter middagen? — Can you wash the dishes after dinner?
Vi dammsuger vardagsrummet varje vecka. — We vacuum the living room every week.
```

Some of these verbs are irregular. **Dammsuga** and **vika** ("to fold") conjugate like
**suga**: dammsuger, dammsög, dammsugit. **Stryka** ("to iron") follows the same
pattern: stryker, strök, strukit. Don't confuse **torka** ("to dry" or "to wipe") with
**damma** ("to dust") — both involve a cloth, but one removes moisture and the other
removes dust.

A useful everyday phrase is **bädda sängen** — "make the bed" — built on the verb
**bädda**. And when something needs to go in the bin, you **slänga** it — "throw it
away" — into the **sopor** (trash, always plural, like English "the dishes").

```example
Bädda sängen innan du går till skolan. — Make the bed before you go to school.
Kom ihåg att slänga soporna ikväll. — Remember to take out the trash tonight.
```

As for nouns: the machines that do the work for you are the **tvättmaskin** (washing
machine) and the **diskmaskin** (dishwasher), while a **dammsugare** (vacuum cleaner)
and a plain **trasa** (rag) handle the rest by hand. All of these individual tasks are
examples of a **hushållssyssla** — a "household chore." Finally, remember the opposite
adjectives **ren** (clean) and **smutsig** (dirty) — both change form with the noun's
gender: rent/rena and smutsigt/smutsiga.
