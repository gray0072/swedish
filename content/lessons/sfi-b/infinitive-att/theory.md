The Swedish infinitive — the "dictionary form" of a verb — usually ends in
**-a**: *äta* (to eat), *läsa* (to read), *simma* (to swim), *sova* (to
sleep), *arbeta* (to work), *resa* (to travel).

The little word **att** links a first verb (or an adjective, or a noun) to an
infinitive. Think of it like the English "to" in "to swim" — except in
Swedish it isn't part of the verb itself, it's a separate connector word.

```example
Att läsa är kul. — Reading is fun. (literally: "To read is fun")
Det är roligt att simma. — It's fun to swim.
Det är viktigt att sova. — It's important to sleep.
```

**Att** also follows many ordinary verbs that introduce another action:

```example
Jag tycker om att läsa. — I like reading.
Jag börjar att laga mat. — I'm starting to cook.
Jag älskar att resa. — I love to travel.
```

There is one important exception you will meet in the next lesson: the modal
verbs **vill** (want to), **kan** (can), **måste** (must) and **får** (may)
are followed directly by the bare infinitive, with **no att**:

```example
Jag vill äta. — I want to eat. (not: Jag vill att äta.)
```

For now, just remember the shape: **att + infinitive** after most verbs,
adjectives ("roligt att...", "viktigt att...") and even a whole sentence
("Att läsa är kul."). The next lesson will show you exactly which verbs skip
the **att**.
