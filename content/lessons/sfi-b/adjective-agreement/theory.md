A Swedish adjective changes its ending to match the noun it describes. You
already know two genders: **en**-words and **ett**-words. With an en-word,
the adjective keeps its plain base form. With an ett-word, it usually adds
**-t**.

```example
en stor bil — a big car
ett stort hus — a big house
en ny bil — a new car
ett nytt hus — a new house
```

This same rule applies when the adjective stands after **är** ("is/are") —
called the predicative position. The adjective still agrees with the subject,
en or ett, exactly the same way as in front of the noun:

```example
Bilen är stor. — The car is big.
Huset är stort. — The house is big.
Hon är snäll. — She is kind.
Barnet är glatt. — The child is happy.
```

So the pattern is: **en**-noun → base form (stor, fin, ny, dyr); **ett**-noun
→ base form **+ t** (stort, fint, nytt, dyrt). It doesn't matter whether the
adjective comes before the noun or after "är" — the ending depends only on
the gender of the noun/subject.

| | en-word | ett-word |
|---|---|---|
| before the noun | en stor bil | ett stort hus |
| after "är" | Bilen är stor. | Huset är stort. |

A quick look ahead: in the plural, adjectives take yet another ending, **-a**,
for both genders — de stora bilarna ("the big cars"), husen är stora ("the
houses are big"). Plural agreement is covered in more detail later; for now,
just recognise that **-a** as "more than one".

```example
En dyr bil. Ett dyrt hus. Bilarna är dyra. — An expensive car. An expensive house. The cars are expensive.
```
