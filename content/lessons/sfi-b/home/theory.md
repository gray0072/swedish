The word **hem** ("home," in the sense of "hearth and home") is different from **hus**
("house" as a building) and **lägenhet** ("apartment"). A Swede would say **Jag är
hemma** ("I'm home") — this is an adverb, not a noun with an article.

```example
Var är köket? — Where is the kitchen?
Jag är i vardagsrummet. — I'm in the living room.
```

Room names are almost always ett-words: **ett kök**, **ett sovrum**, **ett badrum**,
**ett vardagsrum**. Notice that many of them are compound words: **sovrum** literally
means "sleeping room" (from **sova**, "to sleep" + **rum**, "room"), **badrum** means
"bathing room" (from **bada**, "to bathe").

```example
Bordet står vid fönstret. — The table stands by the window.
Soffan är i vardagsrummet. — The sofa is in the living room.
Sängen är stor och bekväm. — The bed is big and comfortable.
```

Furniture and parts of the home are split almost evenly between **en**- and
**ett**-words, so it's best to memorize each word's gender together with the word
itself: **en stol** ("chair"), **en säng** ("bed"), but **ett bord** ("table"), **ett
fönster** ("window"). The definite form (marked by an ending rather than a separate
article) is used when talking about something specific: not just "a table," but "that
particular table standing by the window" — **bordet**.
