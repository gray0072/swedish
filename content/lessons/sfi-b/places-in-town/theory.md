A town is full of useful places, and knowing their names — and their gender —
helps you ask for directions and talk about your day. Most of these words are
**en**-words, but a few common ones are **ett**-words: **ett apotek**
(pharmacy), **ett bibliotek** (library), **ett torg** (square), **ett café**,
**ett gym**, **ett sjukhus** (hospital).

```example
en affär — a shop
en mataffär — a grocery store
ett apotek — a pharmacy
ett bibliotek — a library
ett torg — a square
en park — a park
en bank — a bank
en post — a post office
en kyrka — a church
```

To ask where something is, use the verb **ligga** ("to lie, to be located") in
the question word order: **Var** + verb + subject.

```example
Var ligger apoteket? — Where is the pharmacy?
Var ligger biblioteket? — Where is the library?
Var ligger banken? — Where is the bank?
```

Notice the definite form is used here — "the pharmacy", not "a pharmacy" —
because you are asking about a specific, known place.

To say where you are heading, use **jag ska till** + the definite form:

```example
Jag ska till torget. — I'm going to the square.
Jag ska till apoteket. — I'm going to the pharmacy.
Jag ska till mataffären. — I'm going to the grocery store.
```

Two useful prepositions for describing distance: **nära** (near) and **långt
från** (far from).

```example
Biblioteket ligger nära skolan. — The library is near the school.
Sjukhuset ligger långt från centrum. — The hospital is far from the center.
```

**Centrum** ("the town center") is a special word: it often appears without an
article, as in **i centrum** ("in the center").
