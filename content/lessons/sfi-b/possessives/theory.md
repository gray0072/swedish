You already know **min/mitt** ("my"). Swedish has a matching possessive for
every person, and most of them change form for **en**-words, **ett**-words,
and the plural — exactly like **min/mitt/mina**.

| person | en-word | ett-word | plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| jag (I) | min | mitt | mina |
| du (you) | din | ditt | dina |
| han (he) | hans | hans | hans |
| hon (she) | hennes | hennes | hennes |
| vi (we) | vår | vårt | våra |
| ni (you, pl.) | er | ert | era |
| de (they) | deras | deras | deras |

The important thing to notice: **hans**, **hennes** and **deras** never
change — one form covers en-words, ett-words and plurals alike. Only
**min/din/vår/er** have the three-way split.

```example
Min bil är röd. — My car is red.
Mitt hus är stort. — My house is big.
Mina böcker är nya. — My books are new.

Hans bil är dyr. — His car is expensive.
Hennes hus är stort. — Her house is big.
Deras barn är glada. — Their children are happy.
```

**Vår/vårt/våra** and **er/ert/era** work exactly like **min/mitt/mina** and
**din/ditt/dina** — just for "we" and "you, plural":

```example
Vår bil är gammal. — Our car is old.
Vårt hus är litet. — Our house is small.
Våra vänner kommer idag. — Our friends are coming today.
Er bil står utanför. — Your car is parked outside.
Ert hus är fint. — Your house is nice.
Era barn är trevliga. — Your children are nice.
```

A possessive always goes before the noun, and the noun itself stays in the
indefinite form (no extra "the"-ending): **min bil**, not *min bilen*. Pick
the possessive using two things: whose it is (the person), and the gender or
number of the thing owned.
