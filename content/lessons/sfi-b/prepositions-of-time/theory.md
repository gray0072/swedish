You already know **på** + a weekday: **på måndag** (on Monday). Now build that
into the fuller system Swedish uses for talking about time.

**klockan** ("the clock") introduces a clock time — no extra preposition is
needed before it.

```example
Klockan sju. — Seven o'clock.
Mötet börjar klockan sju. — The meeting starts at seven.
Vad är klockan? — What time is it?
```

**på** covers days and parts of the day — the same word you already know from
weekdays.

```example
på måndag — on Monday
på morgonen — in the morning
Jag springer på morgonen. — I run in the morning.
```

**i** covers a longer stretch of time, especially the current or upcoming
season.

```example
i sommar — this (coming) summer
i vinter — this (coming) winter
Vi ska resa i sommar. — We're going to travel this summer.
```

**om** has two different jobs, and they can look confusingly similar to **i**:
it marks something that repeats regularly, or something that lies a set
distance ahead in the future.

```example
om sommaren — in the summer (every summer, a repeated pattern)
om en vecka — in a week (a week from now)
Om sommaren badar vi ofta. — In the summer we often swim.
Provet är om en vecka. — The test is in a week.
```

Compare **i sommar** and **om sommaren** side by side — the difference is
small in spelling but big in meaning: one summer versus every summer.

| word | use | example |
|---|---|---|
| klockan | clock time | klockan sju |
| på | day, part of day | på måndag, på morgonen |
| i | this coming season | i sommar, i vinter |
| om | repeated time / future distance | om sommaren, om en vecka |
