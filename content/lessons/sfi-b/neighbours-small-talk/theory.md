Swedes don't usually chat for long in the stairwell, but a quick **hälsa** (to say hello) is
expected whenever you pass a **granne** (neighbour) — by the **hiss** (elevator), at the
**brevlåda** (mailbox), or right at your own **dörr** (door). A nod and "hej" is often enough;
if someone stops to **prata** or **småprata** for a minute, that's already a proper little
chat.

```example
Hej! Hur mår du? — Hi! How are you?
Hur står det till? — How are things? / How do you do?
Tack, bra! Och du? — Thanks, good! And you?
```

Everyday stairwell topics are practical: the **portkod** (door code) for a visitor, whether
the **hiss** is working again, which day is cleaning day, or where to find the
**sopnedkast** (trash chute) for the rubbish. Many buildings also have a
**gemensam tvättstuga** (shared laundry room) in the basement, booked on a shared schedule —
a classic place to run into a neighbour and **småprata** for a minute while the machine runs.

```example
Vad är portkoden till huset? — What's the door code for the building?
Vi har en gemensam tvättstuga i källaren. — We have a shared laundry room in the basement.
```

Small talk also covers small favours. You might need to **låna** (borrow) an egg or some
sugar, and you show up and **knacka** (knock) on the door to ask. If you plan to have people
over or make noise, it's polite to warn the neighbours in advance — nobody wants to be
**högljudd** (loud) late at night, since houses in Sweden expect residents to be
reasonably **tyst** (quiet) after around ten in the evening. On the other hand, inviting a
neighbour to **fika** — coffee together with a chat — is a warm, very Swedish way to get to
know someone properly, well beyond a stairwell "hej".

A short exchange ends naturally with something like "Det var **trevligt** att prata med
dig!" ("It was nice talking to you!") — a small, polite close that keeps things pleasant
without turning a two-minute chat into a long conversation. Being a good **granne** in
Sweden is less about deep friendship and more about these small, reliable courtesies:
greeting people, not disturbing (**störa**) them unnecessarily, and helping out when asked.
