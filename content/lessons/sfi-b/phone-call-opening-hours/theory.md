A typical Swedish phone call starts with a greeting and your name: **Hej, det är Sara.**
("Hi, this is Sara.") If you're calling a business, you probably want to know its
**öppettider** (opening hours) — is the shop **öppen** ("open") right now, or already
**stängd** ("closed") for the day?

To ask, use **Vad har ni för öppettider?** ("What are your opening hours?") or simply
**Är det öppet idag?** ("Is it open today?"). If you want to speak to a specific
person, ask **Kan jag få prata med [namn]?** ("May I speak with [name]?"). Whoever
answers might say **Vem söker du?** ("Whom would you like to speak to?"), or ask you
to **vänta** ("wait") a moment: **Ett ögonblick** ("One moment").

```example
Hej, det är Sara. — Hi, this is Sara.
Vad har ni för öppettider? — What are your opening hours?
Vi har öppet till klockan sex. — We're open until six o'clock.
Tack för hjälpen! Hej då! — Thanks for the help! Bye!
```

Sometimes the line is busy: Swedes describe this with **upptaget** — literally
"occupied" — as in *Det är upptaget* ("The line is busy"). If nobody picks up, you
might reach a **telefonsvarare** (answering machine) instead of an answer.

When you explain why you're calling, use **Jag ringer angående...** ("I'm calling
regarding..."). End politely with **Tack för hjälpen!** ("Thanks for the help!")
before you **lägga på** ("hang up") — the opposite of **svara** ("to answer") the
phone.

A short **telefonsamtal** (phone call) like this follows a simple pattern: greet, ask
your question, listen to the answer, thank them, and hang up.

```example
Kan jag få prata med Erik? — May I speak with Erik?
Ett ögonblick... — One moment...
Det är upptaget just nu. — The line is busy right now.
```
