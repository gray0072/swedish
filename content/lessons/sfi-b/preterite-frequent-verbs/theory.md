You already know these six verbs in the present tense. Now meet their past-tense
(preterite) forms — for now, just learn each one as a ready-made word, the way you
learned "är" for "vara". The full system of regular past-tense endings comes later,
in kurs C.

```example
Jag är hemma. — I am home.
Igår var jag hemma. — Yesterday I was home.
```

```example
Jag har en fråga. — I have a question.
Jag hade en fråga. — I had a question.
```

Here are all six pairs together:

| infinitive | present | past | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| vara | är | var | Igår var jag hemma. |
| ha | har | hade | Jag hade en fråga. |
| gå | går | gick | Hon gick till jobbet. |
| komma | kommer | kom | Vi kom sent. |
| säga | säger | sa | Han sa hej. |
| få | får | fick | Jag fick ett brev. |

Notice that none of these forms end in the regular past-tense **-de/-te/-dde**
you may already have seen — that is exactly why they are worth memorizing as a
set. They are also some of the most frequent verbs in everyday Swedish, so you
will meet them constantly once you start telling stories about the past.

A word about time markers: **igår** (yesterday), **i morse** (this morning) and
**förra veckan** (last week) all pair naturally with these past forms.

```example
Igår kväll gick vi på bio. — Last night we went to the cinema.
I morse fick jag ett viktigt mejl. — This morning I got an important email.
Förra veckan var det varmt. — Last week it was warm.
```

One more detail: **säga** has two accepted past forms in writing, **sa** and
**sade** — "sa" is the everyday spoken and written choice, "sade" sounds a
little more formal.

```example
Han sa att han var trött. — He said that he was tired.
```

For now, treat all six forms simply as vocabulary to recognise and use — the
rules for *why* they look this way will make more sense once you learn the
regular preterite patterns.
