You already know **i** (in) and **på** (on/at) for countries, cities and
addresses. The same two words also work for smaller, everyday spaces — and now
you'll add six more prepositions that describe exactly where something is.

```example
Boken är i väskan. — The book is in the bag.
Boken är på bordet. — The book is on the table.
```

Here are the new spatial words, with the picture they paint:

| preposition | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| under | under | Katten ligger under bordet. |
| bakom | behind | Bilen står bakom huset. |
| framför | in front of | Hon står framför tavlan. |
| mellan | between | Skolan ligger mellan banken och biblioteket. |
| över | above, over | Lampan hänger över bordet. |
| bredvid | next to, beside | Stolen står bredvid bordet. |

Notice the pattern: **under** and **över** are opposites (below vs. above), and
so are **bakom** and **framför** (behind vs. in front of). **Mellan** always
needs two things to sit between, joined with **och** (and).

```example
Vi träffas på torget. — We'll meet at the square.
Biblioteket ligger mellan skolan och banken. — The library is between the school and the bank.
Bilen står framför huset, inte bakom det. — The car is in front of the house, not behind it.
```

Two useful verbs go with all of these: **ligga** (to lie, to be located — for
something horizontal or a place) and **stå** (to stand — for something upright).

```example
Staden ligger vid havet. — The town is located by the sea.
Bilen står framför huset. — The car is standing in front of the house.
```

Picture a room or a street corner and describe it out loud — that's the fastest
way to make these words stick.
