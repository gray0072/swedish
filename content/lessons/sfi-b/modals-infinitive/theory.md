In the last lesson you saw that **att** links a verb to an infinitive: *Jag
tycker om att läsa.* Modal verbs are the big exception: **vill** (want to),
**kan** (can, know how to), **måste** (must, have to) and **får** (may, is
allowed to) are followed directly by a bare infinitive — **no att** in
between.

```example
Jag vill sova. — I want to sleep.
Kan du simma? — Can you swim?
Vi måste gå nu. — We must go now.
Du får inte röka här. — You are not allowed to smoke here.
```

Compare the two patterns side by side:

```example
Jag tycker om att läsa. — I like reading. (att + infinitive)
Jag vill läsa. — I want to read. (bare infinitive, no att)
```

A quick guide to meaning:

- **vill** — a wish: *Jag vill äta.* (I want to eat.)
- **kan** — ability or possibility: *Hon kan simma.* (She can swim.)
- **måste** — obligation: *Du måste betala.* (You have to pay.)
- **får** — permission: *Får jag gå?* (May I go?)

To make a modal negative, put **inte** right after it, still before the
infinitive:

```example
Du får inte parkera här. — You are not allowed to park here.
Jag kan inte simma. — I can't swim.
```

And to ask a yes/no question with a modal, simply move it to the front — the
same question word order you already know:

```example
Måste du gå nu? — Do you have to go now?
Får jag röka här? — Am I allowed to smoke here?
```

Note that **måste** has one fixed form for present, and also for the past —
context tells you which is meant.
