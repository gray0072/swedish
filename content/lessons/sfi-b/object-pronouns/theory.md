You already know the subject pronouns — **jag, du, han, hon, vi, ni, de** — the
words that go in front of the verb, doing the action. Now meet their other half:
the **object forms**, used after a verb or after a preposition, when the pronoun
is on the receiving end.

```example
Jag ser dig. — I see you.
Hon ringer mig. — She calls me.
Vi bjuder dem på middag. — We invite them to dinner.
```

Here is the full pairing table — learn it as pairs, not as a list:

| subject | object | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| jag | mig | me |
| du | dig | you |
| han | honom | him |
| hon | henne | her |
| den / det | den / det | it |
| vi | oss | us |
| ni | er | you (plural) |
| de | dem | them |

Notice that **den** and **det** stay the same in both roles — only the "people"
pronouns change shape. Also note the pronunciation trap: **mig** and **dig** are
said "mej" and "dej", and **dem** is said exactly like **de** — "dom".

The object form appears in two places: right after a verb (as its direct or
indirect object), and after any preposition (**till, med, för, om...**).

```example
Läraren hjälper oss. — The teacher helps us.
Kom med oss! — Come with us!
Blommorna är till henne. — The flowers are for her.
```

A simple test: if you can point at the word and ask "whom?" or "to whom?", it's
the object form.

```example
Jag känner honom. — I know him.
Kan du hjälpa mig? — Can you help me?
Vi bjuder er på middag. — We invite you (all) to dinner.
```

One more common trap: after the verb, never use the subject form. Not *Jag ser
han*, but **Jag ser honom.**
