Swedish recipes mostly consist of short verbs in the imperative: **koka** ("boil"),
**stek** ("fry"), **skär** ("cut"). In instructions the imperative usually matches the
stem of the verb — the same stem as in **jag kokar**, just without the ending.

```example
Skär tomaterna i tärningar. — Cut the tomatoes into cubes.
Stek löken i smör. — Fry the onion in butter.
Koka potatisen i femton minuter. — Boil the potatoes for fifteen minutes.
```

Watch out for similar verbs: **skära** — to cut with a knife (skär, skar, skurit),
**hacka** — to chop finely, **skala** — to peel (for example carrots or potatoes),
and **blanda** — simply to mix ingredients in a bowl. When the dish is ready, it's
good to **smaka av** — taste it — and, if needed, add seasoning (**krydda**).

```example
Skala morötterna innan du kokar dem. — Peel the carrots before boiling them.
Smaka av soppan innan du serverar. — Taste the soup before serving it.
```

Useful kitchen words: **kastrull** (pot) for boiling, **stekpanna** (frying pan) for
frying, a sharp **kniv** (knife) and **skärbräda** (cutting board) for cutting, plus
**ugn** (oven) and **spis** (stove) for cooking over heat or baking. A good **recept**
(recipe) usually states the time and temperature.
