Talking about your **fritid** (free time) almost always means talking about verbs — most
Swedish hobby words are simply "to do something": **läsa** (to read), **måla** (to paint),
**rita** (to draw), **sy** (to sew), **sticka** (to knit), **fiska** (to fish), **simma**
(to swim), **dansa** (to dance), **sjunga** (to sing). Nearly all of these are weak verbs
in group 1, conjugating with the familiar -ar/-ade/-at pattern (**målar, målade, målat**).
A few break the pattern: **sy** is a short group-3 verb (**syr, sydde, sytt**), and
**sjunga** is a strong verb with a vowel change (**sjunger, sjöng, sjungit**).

```example
Jag läser en bra bok på kvällarna. — I read a good book in the evenings.
Hon sjunger i en kör. — She sings in a choir.
Vi odlar tomater i trädgården. — We grow tomatoes in the garden.
```

The verb **spela** covers almost any game, sport, or instrument: **spela fotboll** (play
football), **spela ett instrument** (play an instrument), **spela dataspel** (play video
games). Don't confuse it with **leka**, which means "to play" only in the childlike,
make-believe sense (kids playing with toys), never sports or instruments.

Two verbs both translate as "to like": **gilla** and **tycka om**. They're close synonyms,
but **tycka om** is especially common right before an activity introduced by **att**:
**Jag tycker om att simma** ("I like to swim"). To talk about a collecting hobby, use
**samla på**: **Han samlar på frimärken** ("He collects stamps").

```example
Vad tycker du om att göra på fritiden? — What do you like to do in your free time?
Han samlar på frimärken. — He collects stamps.
```

Watch out for **sticka**, which has two unrelated meanings: as a weak verb (**stickar,
stickade, stickat**) it means "to knit"; as a strong verb (**sticker, stack, stuckit**)
it means "to prick" or "to sting". Context makes it clear which one is meant.

Finally, two nouns tie it all together: **hobby** (en hobby, hobbyn, hobbyer, hobbyerna)
and **fritid** (en fritid, fritiden), the free time you spend on them.
