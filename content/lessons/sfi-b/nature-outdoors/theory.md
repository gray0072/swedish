Sweden's outdoors is a big part of everyday life, and a weekend trip out of
town brings its own vocabulary. The core nature words:

```example
en skog — a forest
en sjö — a lake
en strand — a beach, a shore
ett berg — a mountain, a hill
en natur — nature
```

Notice **berg** is an **ett**-word, while the others here are **en**-words.

A few verbs describe what people do outdoors — all group-1 verbs, so they
follow the regular **-ar / -ade / -at** pattern:

```example
cykla — to cycle: cyklar, cyklade, cyklat
simma — to swim: simmar, simmade, simmat
vandra — to hike: vandrar, vandrade, vandrat
grilla — to grill: grillar, grillade, grillat
fiska — to fish: fiskar, fiskade, fiskat
```

Put them together to describe a weekend plan:

```example
Vi tar en lång promenad i skogen. — We take a long walk in the forest.
Vi cyklar till sjön och simmar. — We cycle to the lake and swim.
Vi vandrar i bergen och grillar på kvällen. — We hike in the mountains and grill in the evening.
```

Two handy time phrases for planning a trip: **i helgen** ("this weekend") and
**utanför stan** ("out of town").

```example
Vad ska du göra i helgen? — What are you doing this weekend?
Vi åker utanför stan och andas frisk luft. — We're going out of town and breathing fresh air.
```

Finally, two general nouns that come in handy when describing nature: **ett
träd** (a tree) and **ett djur** (an animal) — both **ett**-words with an
unchanged plural: **träd, träd** and **djur, djur**.

```example
Skogen har många träd och djur. — The forest has many trees and animals.
```
