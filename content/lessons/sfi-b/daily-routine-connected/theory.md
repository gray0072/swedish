A connected account of your day links each action to the next with small words:
**sedan** (then), **därefter** (after that, more formal/written), **efter det**
(after that), **innan** (before), **medan** (while) and **till slut** (finally,
in the end). Vary them instead of repeating "sedan" every time — that is what
makes a story sound natural rather than like a list.

```example
Jag vaknar på morgonen. — I wake up in the morning.
Sedan duschar jag och klär på mig. — Then I shower and get dressed.
Jag äter frukost medan jag lyssnar på nyheterna. — I eat breakfast while I listen to the news.
Därefter borstar jag tänderna och går till jobbet. — After that I brush my teeth and go to work.
```

Swedish also uses **reflexive verbs** for routines: **klä på sig** (get
dressed), **koppla av** (relax — not reflexive itself, but built the same way
around a particle) and **gå och lägga sig** (go to bed, literally built on
**lägga sig**, "lay oneself down"). The tricky part: the reflexive pronoun is
not always **sig** — it changes with the subject, just like "myself/yourself"
in English:

| subject | pronoun | example |
|---|---|---|
| jag | mig | Jag klär på **mig**. |
| du | dig | Du klär på **dig**. |
| han/hon/den/det | sig | Hon klär på **sig**. |
| vi | oss | Vi klär på **oss**. |
| ni | er | Ni klär på **er**. |
| de | sig | De klär på **sig**. |

Only the dictionary (infinitive) form uses **sig** — "klä på sig". In an
actual sentence you must swap it for the pronoun that matches the subject.

For time of day, Swedish slices the day a little finer than English: **på
morgonen** (early morning), **på förmiddagen** (later morning, before noon),
**på eftermiddagen** (afternoon), **på kvällen** (evening) and **på natten**
(night).

```example
På förmiddagen jobbar jag hemifrån. — In the late morning I work from home.
På eftermiddagen hämtar jag barnen. — In the afternoon I pick up the kids.
På kvällen kopplar jag av, och till slut somnar jag. — In the evening I relax, and finally I fall asleep.
```

Put it together and a whole day becomes one flowing account instead of
separate sentences — that is exactly what the quiz will ask you to build.
