Swedish quantity words split into two families depending on the noun: **mass**
nouns (things you don't count one by one, like milk or bread) and **count**
nouns in the **plural** (things you count, like cars or apples).

```example
mycket mjölk — a lot of milk
lite bröd — a little bread
många bilar — many cars
några äpplen — a few apples
```

| quantifier | meaning | pairs with |
|---|---|---|
| mycket | much, a lot of | mass nouns (mjölk, bröd, tid) |
| lite | a little, some | mass nouns (mjölk, bröd, tid) |
| många | many | plural count nouns (bilar, äpplen) |
| några | some, a few | plural count nouns (bilar, äpplen) |
| alla | all, everyone | plural nouns (barn, bilar) |

**Alla** works with any plural noun, regardless of what it's counting:

```example
Alla barn tycker om glass. — All children like ice cream.
Alla bilar är röda. — All the cars are red.
```

The negative quantifier "no/none" is special: it agrees with the noun's
gender and number, just like "en/ett/plural" — so it comes in three forms:
**ingen** (en-word, singular), **inget** (ett-word, singular), **inga**
(any word, plural).

```example
Jag har ingen bil. — I don't have a car. (en bil)
Vi har inget bröd. — We have no bread. (ett bröd)
Det finns inga bilar här. — There are no cars here. (bilar, plural)
```

A quick way to remember: if you'd say "en/ett" for the noun in the singular,
match that with **ingen/inget**; if the noun is plural, use **inga**.

```example
en bil → ingen bil
ett hus → inget hus
bilar → inga bilar
```

Putting it together in one short story:

```example
Vi har lite mjölk men inget bröd hemma. — We have a little milk but no
bread at home.
Det finns många bilar men inga cyklar på gatan. — There are many cars but
no bikes on the street.
```
