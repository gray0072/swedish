At the **bibliotek** — library — you can **låna** books, newspapers and magazines for
free, once you have a **lånekort**, a library card you get simply by registering at
the desk. Every **bok** (plural **böcker**) has a due date, and if you keep it past
that date you may owe a **förseningsavgift** — a late fee.

```example
Jag vill låna den här boken. — I want to borrow this book.
Du måste lämna tillbaka boken på fredag. — You have to return the book on Friday.
```

Besides books, most libraries carry a **tidning** (newspaper) and a **tidskrift**
(magazine), kept on a **hylla** near the entrance so anyone can **bläddra** — leaf
through — them without borrowing anything at all. If the book you want is out, you
can **reservera** it: the librarian sets it aside for you as soon as it comes back.
And if you need more time to finish **läsa** — reading — something, most libraries
let you **förnya** the loan, either online or at the desk, often more than once.

```example
Jag ska bläddra i en tidskrift. — I'm going to leaf through a magazine.
Kan jag förnya lånet en gång till? — Can I renew the loan one more time?
```

Every library posts its **öppettider** — opening hours — which vary from day to day,
and is usually split into an **avdelning**, or two, such as a children's section or a
corner with **e-bok** terminals for digital reading. Inside, the basic rule is to be
**tyst** — quiet — so that everyone can read and work undisturbed. If you can't find
something, just ask the **bibliotekarie** — the librarian is exactly the person to
help.
