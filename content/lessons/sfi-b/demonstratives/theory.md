You already know **det här** and **det där** for "this is" / "that is" as a
standalone statement (Det här är min bil. — This is my car.). Now let's use
demonstratives directly in front of a noun, to say "this car" or "that
house".

The demonstrative has to agree with the noun's gender and number, just like
adjectives and possessives do: **den här** with an en-word, **det här** with
an ett-word, **de här** with a plural. The same split applies to "that" —
**den där / det där / de där**.

```example
den här bilen — this car
det här huset — this house
de här bilarna — these cars

den där bilen — that car
det där huset — that house
de där husen — those houses
```

Notice the noun itself: it takes the **definite** ending (-en, -et, -na/-en),
exactly as if you had said "the car" — **bilen**, not *bil*. Swedish
double-marks definiteness here: the demonstrative word up front, and the
"the"-ending on the noun, both at once. You cannot drop either one:

```example
Den här bilen är ny. — This car is new.
Det där huset är litet. — That house is small.
De här bilarna är dyra. — These cars are expensive.
```

A quick summary:

| | this (här) | that (där) |
|---|---|---|
| en-word | den här bilen | den där bilen |
| ett-word | det här huset | det där huset |
| plural | de här bilarna | de där husen |

So the rule is simple: pick **den/det/de** by gender and number, add **här**
for "this" or **där** for "that", and put the noun after it in its definite
form.
