Swedish has three everyday verbs for "to like/love", and choosing between them
is mostly about how strong the feeling is. All three work the same way
grammatically: they can be followed by a **noun**, or by **att + infinitive**
(an activity).

```example
Jag tycker om choklad. — I like chocolate.
Jag tycker om att simma. — I like to swim.
```

**tycker om** — neutral, safe in both speech and writing; the default choice
when you are not sure which to pick.

**gillar** — a shorter, more casual synonym, very common in everyday speech
(and only one word, unlike "tycker om").

**älskar** — much stronger: real enthusiasm or love, not just mild fondness.
Use it when you truly love something, the way "love" is stronger than "like"
in English.

```example
Jag gillar choklad. — I like chocolate. (casual)
Jag tycker om att läsa. — I like to read. (neutral)
Hon älskar att laga mat. — She loves to cook. (strong)
```

The pattern with a noun and with an infinitive works identically for all
three:

| verb | + noun | + att + infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| tycker om | Jag tycker om musik. | Jag tycker om att läsa. |
| gillar | Jag gillar glass. | Jag gillar att spela fotboll. |
| älskar | Hon älskar choklad. | Hon älskar att laga mat. |

A short scale, from mild to strong: **tycker om / gillar** (like) → **älskar**
(love). None of them change form for "I/you/he/she/we/they" — like every
Swedish verb in the present tense, one form fits all.

```example
Vi tycker om att simma på sommaren. — We like to swim in the summer.
Han gillar musik. — He likes music.
Alla barn älskar glass. — All children love ice cream.
```
