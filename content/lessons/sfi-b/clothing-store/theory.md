When you shop for clothes in Sweden, the first question is usually about **storlek**
("size"). If you're unsure, ask to **prova** something — try it on — in the
**omklädningsrum** (fitting room). Once you have it on, the real question is whether it
**passar**: fits. Something can be **för stor** (too big) or **för liten** (too small),
and both phrases work with any item of clothing.

```example
Vilken storlek har du? — What size are you?
Kan jag prova den här klänningen? — Can I try on this dress?
Byxorna passar inte, de är för trånga. — The pants don't fit, they're too tight.
```

Don't confuse **prova** and **passa**: prova is the action of trying something on,
while passa describes the result — whether it actually fits. You can prova a sweater
and find out it doesn't passa at all.

If a piece of clothing doesn't work out, you can usually **byta** it — exchange it for
another size — or **returnera** it, get your money back. For either, keep the
**kvitto** (receipt); shops normally ask for it. Watch for signs saying **rea**
(sale) or offering a **rabatt** (discount) — a good way to save money on a new
**tröja** or pair of shoes.

```example
Jag vill byta den här tröjan mot en större. — I'd like to exchange this sweater for a bigger one.
Du kan returnera kläderna inom trettio dagar. — You can return the clothes within thirty days.
Skorna var på rea, jag betalade halva priset. — The shoes were on sale, I paid half price.
```

When you're ready to pay, head to the **kassa** (checkout), where you **betala**
(pay) for what you've chosen. If you need help finding the right size or a specific
item, ask the **expedit** (shop assistant) — that's their job when you go **handla**
(shopping) for clothes.
