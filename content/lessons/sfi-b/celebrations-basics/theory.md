Sweden marks its **högtider** (holidays) with distinct traditions throughout the year.
The two biggest are **jul** (Christmas) in December and **midsommar** (Midsummer) in
June — opposite in mood and season, but both central to Swedish culture.

At **jul**, families decorate a **julgran** (Christmas tree) with **ljus**
(candles/lights) and ornaments, exchange **presenter** (gifts), and share a **tårta**
or other treats together. Swedish Christmas Eve, *julafton* on December 24, is when
most of the celebration actually happens — earlier than in many other countries.

```example
Vi firar jul med familjen. — We celebrate Christmas with the family.
Barnen öppnar sina presenter på julafton. — The children open their gifts on Christmas Eve.
```

**Midsommar**, held on a Friday in late June, celebrates the long daylight of the
Swedish summer. People raise a **midsommarstång** (maypole) and **dansa** (dance)
around it, **sjunga** (sing) traditional songs, and often wear flower wreaths.

```example
Man reser en midsommarstång på midsommarafton. — People raise a maypole on Midsummer's Eve.
Vi sjunger och dansar runt stången. — We sing and dance around the pole.
```

Other **högtider** worth knowing: **påsk** (Easter), when children paint eggs, and
**nyår** (New Year), celebrated with fireworks at midnight. A **födelsedag**
(birthday) is a smaller, personal högtid — you might get an **inbjudan**
(invitation) to a party, receive a **present**, and hear everyone say **grattis**
("congratulations!" — the standard Swedish birthday greeting).

To **fira** (celebrate) something, you simply name what you're celebrating: *fira
jul*, *fira midsommar*, *fira en födelsedag*. And if you want company, you
**bjuder in** (invite) guests ahead of time.
