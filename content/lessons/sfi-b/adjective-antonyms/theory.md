Swedish adjectives change their ending depending on the noun they describe: with an
**en**-word the adjective keeps its base form, with an **ett**-word it usually adds
**-t**, and in the plural (either gender) it usually adds **-a**. This is called
agreement, and it works the same way for every antonym pair below.

Compare **stor** ("big") with its opposite **liten** ("small"): en stor bil, ett stort
hus, stora bilar — but liten is irregular: en liten bil, ett litet hus, and the plural
is simply små bilar.

```example
Huset är stort, men trädgården är liten. — The house is big, but the garden is small.
Elefanten är stor, men musen är liten. — The elephant is big, but the mouse is small.
```

Most pairs follow the regular pattern. **Ny** ("new") becomes nytt/nya, and its
opposite **gammal** ("old") becomes gammalt/gamla — nothing is dropped, the endings
are simply added. **Snabb** ("fast") becomes snabbt/snabba, while **långsam** ("slow")
becomes långsamt/långsamma (the m doubles before -a, just like in "samma").

A few pairs deserve extra attention. **Öppen** ("open") is irregular in the same way
as liten: it becomes öppet in the ett-form and öppna in the plural, not "öppent" —
remember it alongside vaken → vaket → vakna. Its opposite, **stängd** ("closed"), is
perfectly regular: stängt, stängda.

```example
Butiken är öppen på dagen men stängd på natten. — The shop is open during the day but closed at night.
Glaset är fullt, men flaskan är tom. — The glass is full, but the bottle is empty.
```

Watch for adjectives that end in a single consonant after a short vowel, like **tom**
("empty") and **full** ("full"): the consonant doubles before -a — tomma, fulla — the
same rule you just saw in långsamma.

Whenever you describe an object, the adjective must agree with its noun in gender and
number, even though English never changes "big" or "small" at all. Learning antonym
pairs together — stor/liten, lång/kort, dyr/billig, ren/smutsig, hög/låg, bred/smal —
is a fast way to double your vocabulary while training this agreement pattern at the
same time.
