In Sweden almost every small child spends the day at a **förskola** (preschool)
before starting **skola** (school) around age six. At preschool, children don't
necessarily have a "lärare" in the strict sense — they often call the staff
**fröken**, an old but still common word children use to address a teacher.

Everyday life follows a steady rhythm: a **skoldag** starts when a parent
**lämnar** (drops off) the child in the morning and ends when someone
**hämtar** (picks up) the child later. In between there is a **rast** (a
break) to play with a **klasskompis** (classmate), a **mellanmål** (snack),
and often a **vila** (rest) after lunch. There is also **samling**, a circle
time when the children sit together, sing and hear the plan for the day.

```example
Mamma lämnar mig på förskolan på morgonen. — Mom drops me off at preschool in the morning.
Pappa hämtar mig klockan fyra. — Dad picks me up at four o'clock.
Jag måste göra min läxa innan middagen. — I have to do my homework before dinner.
```

Once real schoolwork begins, new words appear: **läxa** (homework) to do at
home, and a **skolväska** to carry books in. Outside of the daily routine,
**barn** (children) sometimes go on a **utflykt** (an outing) or play at the
**lekplats** (playground) — always with a **förälder** (parent) or teacher
nearby.

```example
Barnen leker på lekplatsen efter lunch. — The children play on the playground after lunch.
Klassen ska på utflykt till skogen. — The class is going on a trip to the forest.
```

One practical phrase every parent needs: if a child is unwell, you must
**sjukanmäla** them — report them sick — usually through an app or by phone,
before the school day starts.
