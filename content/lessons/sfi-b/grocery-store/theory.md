When you shop for food in Sweden, you usually start with a **kundvagn** (shopping cart) or a **korg** (basket) if you're only buying a few things. Fresh food is organized into an **avdelning** (department): look for **frukt** and **grönsak** near the entrance, **kött** further in, and **mejeri** (dairy) products like milk and cheese along the cold shelves. **Bröd** (bread) has its own section too.

Some fruit and vegetables need to be weighed before you check out — **väga** them yourself in the produce area and print a sticker, or the cashier will weigh them at the **kassa** (checkout).

```example
Du måste väga bananerna innan du betalar. — You need to weigh the bananas before paying.
Ta en korg innan du handlar. — Take a basket before you shop.
```

At busy times there can be a long **kö** (queue) at the checkout. When it's your turn, you **betala** (pay) — either **kontant** (in cash) or with a **kort** (card); most Swedes pay by card. Ask for a **påse** (bag) if you need one — many stores charge a small fee for it.

Watch for signs with a **rabatt** (discount) or a special **erbjudande** (offer) — these mean the price is temporarily lower. Keep your **kvitto** (receipt): if something is wrong with an item, you can **byta** (exchange) it later, usually only with the receipt as proof of purchase.

```example
Jag ska köpa bröd och mjölk. — I'm going to buy bread and milk.
Du kan byta varan om du har kvittot. — You can exchange the item if you have the receipt.
```

Two useful verbs to keep apart: **handla** means "to shop, to go shopping" in general (Vi handlar på lördagar — We go shopping on Saturdays), while **köpa** means "to buy" a specific thing (Jag köper mjölk — I buy milk).
