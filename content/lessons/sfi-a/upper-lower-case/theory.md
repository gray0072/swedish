Every letter of the Swedish alphabet exists in two shapes: **stor bokstav**
(capital letter) and **liten bokstav** (lower-case letter). Handwriting mixes
them the same way print does — you only need to know *when* to switch.

```example
Anna bor i Malmö. — Anna lives in Malmö.
```

Here **Anna** and **Malmö** both start with a capital letter because they are
names — of a person and of a place. Everything else in the sentence is
lower-case. That single rule already covers most of what you need: names of
people, countries, cities and streets always start with **stor bokstav**.

```example
Mitt namn är Ali. Jag bor på Kungsgatan. — My name is Ali. I live on Kungsgatan.
```

When you fill in a form by hand, you are often asked to write **textat** —
in clear, separated block letters rather than joined cursive — so that anyone
can read it, including a computer that scans the form later.

```example
Skriv textat, tack. — Please write in block letters.
Skriv ditt namn här, tack. — Write your name here, please.
```

Practise writing your own name several times, first slowly, letter by letter,
then as one flowing word — both the capital first letter and the lower-case
rest of it.

```example
A-n-n-a. Anna.
```

A form usually ends with a line for your **underskrift** — your signature —
which does not have to look like careful block letters; it is your own,
personal way of writing your name.

```example
Formuläret behöver din underskrift. — The form needs your signature.
```
