This is the first lesson where you tell a whole story instead of a single
sentence. It rests on three linking words: **först** (first), **sedan** (then)
and **till slut** (in the end). In speech "sedan" is often shortened to **sen**.

```example
Först vaknar jag. — First I wake up.
Sedan äter jag frukost. — Then I eat breakfast.
Sen går jag till skolan. — Then I go to school.
Till slut går jag och lägger mig. — In the end I go to bed.
```

Look closely at those sentences. You start with a word about time — and the verb
comes right after it, while **jag** moves behind the verb. Not *Först jag
vaknar*, but **Först vaknar jag**. Copy that shape exactly as it is:

| start | verb | jag | rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Först | vaknar | jag | |
| Sedan | äter | jag | frukost |
| På morgonen | dricker | jag | kaffe |
| På kvällen | tittar | jag | på tv |

If you start with "jag", everything is ordinary: `Jag äter frukost på morgonen.`
Both versions are correct — just pick one and do not mix them.

Here is the whole day, following the pattern:

```example
Jag vaknar klockan sju och går upp. — I wake up at seven and get up.
Sedan äter jag frukost och går till skolan. — Then I eat breakfast and go to school.
Klockan tolv äter jag lunch. — At twelve I eat lunch.
På kvällen lagar jag mat och äter middag. — In the evening I cook and eat dinner.
```

All of this is the present tense — "every day". For the past, it is enough for
now just to recognise the two most frequent forms when you hear them:

```example
Igår gick jag till skolan. — Yesterday I went to school.
Igår åt jag middag klockan sex. — Yesterday I ate dinner at six.
```
