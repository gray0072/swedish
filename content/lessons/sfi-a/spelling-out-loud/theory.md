You already know the alphabet. Now use it for something practical: telling a
name or an address letter by letter, when someone cannot understand you the
normal way — on the phone, at a counter, or when a word is unusual.

```example
Hur stavas ditt namn? — How do you spell your name?
Jag stavar mitt namn: A-N-N-A. — I'll spell my name: A-N-N-A.
```

If you did not catch a name or a word, ask the other person to spell it, or to
repeat it:

```example
Kan du bokstavera det? — Can you spell that out?
Kan du upprepa det, tack? — Can you repeat that, please?
```

A first name is not the whole story — Swedish forms often ask for **förnamn**
(first name) and **efternamn** (last name) separately.

```example
Vad heter du i efternamn? — What is your last name?
Det stavas med två s. — It's spelled with two s.
```

Street names can be just as tricky, especially with **å, ä, ö** in them —
so it helps to name the letter clearly and say whether it is capital or small.

```example
Hur stavas gatan du bor på? — How do you spell the street you live on?
Är det å, ä eller ö? — Is it å, ä or ö?
Stort S, som i Sara. — Capital S, as in Sara.
```

When someone writes down what you say, give them a moment:

```example
Vänta lite, jag skriver ner det. — Wait a moment, I'll write it down.
```

Put together, a whole exchange looks like this:

```example
— Hur stavas ditt namn?
— A-N-N-A. Stort A.
— Och efternamn?
— Lindqvist. L-I-N-D-Q-V-I-S-T.
— Kan du upprepa det, tack?
— Ja, L-I-N-D-Q-V-I-S-T.
```
