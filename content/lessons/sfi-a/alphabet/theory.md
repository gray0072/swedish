Det svenska alfabetet (шведский алфавит) has **29 letters**. The first 26 are the same as in English: a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i, j, k, l, m, n, o, p, q, r, s, t, u, v, w, x, y, z.

Then come three EXTRA letters at the very end: **å, ä, ö**. This is important: å is not "a with a ring", ä is not "a with dots", and ö is not "o with dots" — in Swedish they are separate letters with their own place in the alphabet, right after z. A Swedish dictionary or a phone book always sorts ... x, y, z, å, ä, ö at the end, so if you're looking up a word starting with one of these, look last, not among the a's or o's.

Sound-wise:
- **å** sounds like the "o" in English "more" — e.g. **båt** (boat), **gå** (to walk).
- **ä** sounds like the "e" in English "bed" — e.g. **äta** (to eat), **väska** (bag).
- **ö** sounds like the "u" in French "tu" or German ö — e.g. **sjö** (lake), **öra** (ear).

These three letters change the meaning of a word completely, so mixing them up with a/o can turn one word into another. Practice hearing and typing them — most Swedish keyboards have å, ä, ö as separate keys right next to å (top-right area).

A few consonant combinations sound different from what you'd expect: **sj**, **skj**, and **stj** all make a similar "sh"-like sound (as in **sjö**, **skjorta**), and soft **g**/**k** before e, i, y, ä, ö sound like "y" or "sh" (as in **gärna**, **köpa**).

```example
Han bor på en ö. — Он живёт на острове.
Vi äter äpplen i trädgården. — Мы едим яблоки в саду.
Sjön är vacker på sommaren. — Озеро красиво летом.
Vad heter du? Kan du bokstavera det? — Как тебя зовут? Можешь произнести по буквам?
```

Being able to spell your name out loud — bokstavera — is one of the first practical skills you need in Sweden: on the phone, at the vårdcentral (health center), or filling out a form together with someone. Learn the letters well, especially å, ä, ö, and you'll be able to spell anything.
