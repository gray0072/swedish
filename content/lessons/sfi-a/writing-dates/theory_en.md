In Sweden a date is written **from big to small**: first the year, then the month,
then the day.

```example
2026-09-07 — 7 September 2026
```

This is the standard format on forms, in letters from the authorities, at the hospital
and at the bank. It differs from both the Russian order (07.09.2026) and the American
one (09/07/2026). So remember the key point: **the first number is the year**.

In running text you write it out: **den 7 september 2026**. The month is in lower case,
as always in Swedish.

## How the date is said aloud

Out loud, the little word **den** comes before the number, and the number becomes an
ordinal:

```example
den sjunde september — the seventh of September
den första maj — the first of May
den trettioförsta december — the thirty-first of December
```

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| första | andra | tredje | fjärde | femte | sjätte |

| 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |

| sjunde | åttonde | nionde | tionde | elfte | tolfte |

| 20 | 21 | 30 | 31 |
|---|---|---|---|
| tjugonde | tjugoförsta | trettionde | trettioförsta |

```example
Vilket datum är det idag? — What is the date today?
Idag är det den sjunde september. — Today is the seventh of September.
Imorgon är det den åttonde september. — Tomorrow is the eighth of September.
```

## Vecka 37

Sweden runs on **week numbers**. They follow the ISO 8601 standard: a week starts on
Monday, and a year has 52 or 53 of them. At work and at school nobody says "early
September" — they say **vecka 37**, or **v. 37** for short. The week number is printed
in every Swedish calendar.

```example
Vi ses vecka 37. — See you in week 37.
Semestern börjar v. 28. — The holiday starts in week 28.
```

Three words hold the whole system together: **vecka**, **månad**, **år**.
