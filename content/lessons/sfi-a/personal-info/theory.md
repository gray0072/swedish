When you visit a Swedish authority — Skatteverket (tax agency), Försäkringskassan (social insurance agency), or a vårdcentral (health center) — you always fill in the same kind of **blankett** (form) with **personuppgifter** (personal information). Here are the words you need.

Your **namn** (name) splits into two parts: **förnamn** (first name) and **efternamn** (surname). On the form you also write your **adress** (address) together with a **postnummer** (postal code).

The single most important number in Sweden is your **personnummer** — ten digits that identify you everywhere, written like `990101-1234`. You'll also be asked for your **telefonnummer** (phone number), your **födelsedatum** (date of birth), your **nationalitet** (nationality), and your **medborgarskap** (citizenship).

Other common fields: **kön** (gender — man/kvinna), **civilstånd** (marital status — gift/ogift/skild, i.e. married/single/divorced), and **yrke** (occupation — what you work with).

At the bottom of the form you must **underteckna** (sign) it — that means writing your **underskrift** (signature). If a field is unclear, just ask what it means; every clerk is used to helping with this.

```example
Hur stavas ditt efternamn? — How is your surname spelled?
Vad är ditt personnummer? — What is your personal number?
Kan du fylla i den här blanketten? — Can you fill in this form?
Jag heter Anna Andersson. — My name is Anna Andersson.
```

Knowing these words by heart saves a lot of time — and stress — on your first visit to a Swedish authority. A tip: **fylla i** means "to fill in" (a form), while **skriva under** and **underteckna** both mean "to sign" — the second is a bit more formal and is the word you'll see printed on the form itself, right above the signature line.
