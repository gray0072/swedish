A Swedish phone number is almost always said **digit by digit**. The point of this
lesson is not counting — it is hearing and saying digits quickly.

A mobile number starts with **07**, for example 073-123 45 67. The beginning is read
one digit at a time: *noll sju tre*. You can carry on the same way — or read the rest
**in pairs**, where each pair becomes one word: 45 is *fyrtiofem*, 67 is *sextiosju*,
73 is *sjuttiotre*.

The whole tens are worth learning by heart:

| 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tjugo | trettio | fyrtio | femtio | sextio | sjuttio | åttio | nittio |

**Riktnummer** is the area code: Stockholm 08, Göteborg 031, Malmö 040.
**Landsnummer** is the country code, and Sweden's is **+46**. The **+** sign is called
*plus*, and the **-** sign is a *bindestreck*.

```example
Vad är ditt telefonnummer? — What is your phone number?
Kan du upprepa numret? — Can you repeat the number?
Kan du säga det långsammare? — Can you say it more slowly?
Jag stavar mitt namn. — I spell my name.
```

## Personnummer

A **personnummer** is your personal identity number. The format is **YYMMDD-XXXX** —
six digits for the date of birth, then a hyphen and four more digits. Ten digits in
all. The modern long form also writes the century: **YYYYMMDD-XXXX**.

Here is an example (invented, not a real number): **900101-1234**. Out loud it sounds
like this: *nio noll noll ett noll ett, bindestreck, ett två tre fyra*.

A personnummer is always read **one digit at a time**, never in pairs. You are asked
for it at the doctor, at the bank and at the pharmacy — almost every time you walk in
somewhere.

```example
Kan jag få ditt personnummer? — Can I have your personal identity number?
Ring mig imorgon. — Call me tomorrow.
```

If you did not catch it, do not stay silent. Say *Kan du upprepa numret?* or
*Kan du säga det långsammare?* That is normal and polite.
