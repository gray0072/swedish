Five small words carry most of everyday politeness in Swedish. Learn them as
ready-made reactions, not as grammar.

```example
Tack! — Thanks!
Tack så mycket! — Thank you very much!
Varsågod! — You're welcome! / Here you go!
Förlåt! — Sorry! (asking forgiveness)
Ursäkta! — Excuse me!
```

**Tack** answers almost anything good that happens to you — a gift, help, a
compliment. **Varsågod** is the mirror word: it is what *you* say back, or say
when you hand something over or invite someone to sit or eat.

```example
— Tack för hjälpen! — Varsågod!
— Här är din kaffe. Varsågod! — Tack!
```

**Förlåt** and **ursäkta** both translate as "sorry", but they are used
differently. **Förlåt** is for something you did wrong — you bumped into
someone, you are late. **Ursäkta** is for getting someone's attention or
getting past them — it is what you say *before* you ask a stranger something.

```example
Förlåt, jag är sen. — Sorry, I'm late.
Ursäkta, var är toaletten? — Excuse me, where is the toilet?
Ursäkta mig, får jag komma förbi? — Excuse me, may I get past?
```

If someone apologises to you, the easy reply is **ingen fara** — "no
problem", literally "no danger".

```example
— Förlåt! — Ingen fara.
```

To ask for something politely, add **tack** at the end of a short request, or
use **Kan jag få …?** ("May I have …?").

```example
En kaffe, tack. — A coffee, please.
Kan jag få en kaffe, tack? — May I have a coffee, please?
```
