In a Swedish shop almost the whole conversation runs on one question and one
answer.

**Vad kostar det?** — "How much does it cost?" People just as often say
**Hur mycket kostar det?** The answer always follows one pattern: **Det kostar**
+ a number + **kronor**.

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Vad kostar det? | Det kostar tio kronor. |
| Vad kostar boken? | Den kostar tjugo kronor. |
| Hur mycket kostar det? | Det kostar femtio kronor. |

```example
Vad kostar det? — How much does it cost?
Det kostar femton kronor. — It costs fifteen kronor.
Jag tar den här. — I'll take this one.
```

Swedish money is **kronor**; a single coin is **en krona**. Price tags write
**kr**, banks and websites write **SEK**: "20 kr" = "20 SEK" = twenty kronor.

**Card, not cash.** Sweden has almost given up cash. In shops, on buses and in
cafés people pay by card or phone, and a sign on the door often says
**Vi tar inte kontanter** — "We don't take cash." So this is the most useful
phrase here:

```example
Kan jag betala med kort? — Can I pay by card?
Jag betalar med kort. — I am paying by card.
Kan jag få ett kvitto? — Can I have a receipt?
```

Note that **ett kvitto** is an ett-word, even though a receipt feels like the
most ordinary object there is. Not "en kvitto". And **kontant** ("in cash")
comes after the verb: **Jag betalar kontant.**

If you are only browsing, say **Jag vill bara titta.** At the till you will be
asked **Vill du ha en påse?** — answer **Ja, tack** or **Nej, tack**. And right
at the end: **Tack, det var allt.**
