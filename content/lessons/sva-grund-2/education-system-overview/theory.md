# The Swedish education system — an overview

The Swedish education system has several stages. Children attend **förskola** (preschool) from about one to six years old, then spend nine years in **grundskola** — compulsory school for every child in the country.

```example
Mitt barn går på förskolan tre dagar i veckan. — My child goes to preschool three days a week.
Grundskolan är obligatorisk för alla barn i Sverige. — Compulsory school is mandatory for all children in Sweden.
```

After that, most students move on to **gymnasium** — three-year upper secondary school. It isn't legally required, but it's needed for almost any further studies. Adults often start over at **komvux** — municipal adult education, where you can raise missing grades or gain **behörighet** (eligibility) for a **högskola** or **universitet**. The difference between the two is small: a **högskola** is usually smaller and more specialized, while a **universitet** is larger and has the right to award doctoral degrees.

```example
Jag läser upp mina betyg på komvux för att få behörighet till högskolan. — I'm raising my grades at komvux to get eligibility for higher education.
Vilken skillnad är det mellan en högskola och ett universitet? — What's the difference between a university college and a university?
```

Grades in gymnasium and higher education follow a **betygssystem** (grading system) from A to F, where F means "not passed". The sum of your grade points is called your **meritvärde**, and it's exactly what decides **antagning** (admission) to popular programs when there are more applicants than places.

```example
Betygssystemet går från A till F. — The grading system runs from A to F.
Mitt meritvärde avgör om jag blir antagen till programmet. — My merit rating decides whether I get admitted to the program.
```

Some choose a **yrkesutbildning** (vocational education) instead of an academic program. And to apply to certain higher-education programs, you can additionally take the **högskoleprovet** — a standardized test that can improve your chances regardless of your school grades.
