Describing statistics and trends in a text — a chart, a survey, a news
report — calls for a small set of quantifying phrases, beyond plain
numbers.

## Fractions and shares

```example
De flesta arbetar heltid. — Most people work full-time.
En tredjedel av eleverna klarade provet. — A third of the students passed the test.
Andelen unga som studerar har ökat. — The share of young people studying has increased.
Ungefär hälften svarade ja. — Roughly half answered yes.
```

**Andel** ("share, proportion") is the noun form used when a text talks
about a changing percentage over time — it pairs naturally with **öka**
(increase) and **minska** (decrease).

```example
Andelen minskade förra året. — The share decreased last year.
```

## Majority and minority

```example
En majoritet röstade för förslaget. — A majority voted for the proposal.
En minoritet var emot. — A minority were against.
En liten del av befolkningen berörs. — A small part of the population is affected.
```

## Describing a trend over time

**Allt fler** and its opposite **allt färre** describe a growing or
shrinking number of people or things over time — a common phrase in
describing a chart's direction.

```example
Allt fler väljer att cykla till jobbet. — An increasing number of people choose to cycle to work.
Allt färre röker idag jämfört med för tjugo år sedan. — Fewer and fewer people smoke today compared to twenty years ago.
```

## In short

Reach for **de flesta / en majoritet** for "most", a fraction like **en
tredjedel** for an exact share, **andelen** with **öka/minska** for a
changing percentage, and **allt fler / allt färre** for a trend moving in
one direction.
