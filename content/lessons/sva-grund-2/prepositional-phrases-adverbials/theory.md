A single preposition plus a noun phrase can act as one **adverbial** —
adding when, why, or how something happened, without a subordinate clause
at all.

## Cause: på grund av, till följd av

```example
Tåget var sent på grund av regnet. — The train was late because of the
rain.
Priserna steg till följd av kriget. — Prices rose as a result of the war.
```

**På grund av** is the everyday choice; **till följd av** sounds more
formal, common in news and analytical writing — both do the same job a
clause with **eftersom** could do, but more compactly.

```example
Tåget var sent på grund av regnet. — a compact phrase
Tåget var sent eftersom det regnade. — the same idea as a full clause
```

## Manner: med, utan

```example
Hon tog emot priset med stor glädje. — She accepted the prize with great
joy.
Han svarade utan tvekan. — He answered without hesitation.
```

## Time: under, vid

```example
Vi bodde där under hela sommaren. — We lived there throughout the whole
summer.
Hon var på plats vid tillfället. — She was on the scene at the time.
```

## Why phrases instead of clauses

A prepositional phrase is shorter and often reads more smoothly than a full
subordinate clause, especially when the extra detail is secondary to the
sentence's main point — a useful tool for tightening a paragraph without
losing information.

```example
Mötet flyttades på grund av vädret. — The meeting was moved because of the
weather. (one tight phrase, not a whole "eftersom det var dåligt väder")
```

## In short

Treat a whole "preposition + noun phrase" as a single building block: it
slots into the sentence just like a single adverb, and lets you pack a
reason, a manner, or a time span into a few compact words.
