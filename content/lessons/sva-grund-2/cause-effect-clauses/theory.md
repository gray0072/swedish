Cause is expressed with **eftersom** and **därför att** — both introduce a
full bisats with a subject and a finite verb, and BIFF applies inside it.
Result (consequence) is expressed with **så att**. But **på grund av** and
**tack vare** take not a bisats but a plain noun phrase — there's no finite
verb of their own there.

## eftersom / därför att — cause, a full bisats

```example
Vi stannade hemma eftersom det regnade. — We stayed home because it was raining.
Han är trött därför att han jobbade hela natten. — He's tired because he worked all night.
```

Both translate as "because"/"since" and work the same way: conjunction +
subject + finite verb. With negation, following BIFF:

```example
Vi stannade hemma eftersom det inte slutade regna. — We stayed home because it didn't stop raining.
```

## så att — result

**så att** doesn't introduce a cause but the result of what was just said:

```example
Han talade högt, så att alla kunde höra. — He spoke loudly, so that everyone could hear.
```

## på grund av and tack vare — a noun phrase only

**på grund av** ("because of") and **tack vare** ("thanks to") take not a
bisats but a noun phrase — with no finite verb of their own:

```example
Skolan stängde på grund av snön. — The school closed because of the snow.
Hon klarade provet tack vare sin lärare. — She passed the exam thanks to her teacher.
```

**tack vare** usually points to a positive cause ("thanks to"), while
**på grund av** is neutral or negative ("because of").

## The test

If a subject and a finite verb follow the word — eftersom/därför att/så att,
and BIFF applies. If a plain noun phrase follows with no verb of its own —
på grund av or tack vare.
