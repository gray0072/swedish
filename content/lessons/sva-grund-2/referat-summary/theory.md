When you write a **referat** (a written summary of someone else's text), rule number one
is to stay **objektiv** ("objective") and write in the third person, not the first. A
personal opinion ("jag tycker att...", "I think that...") doesn't belong in a referat —
it's not an essay, but a neutral summary of an article's content.

```example
Artikeln handlar om klimatförändringar i Sverige. — The article is about climate change in Sweden.
Texten tar upp frågan om vattenbrist. — The text raises the question of water shortage.
```

To convey the author's position, use the verbs **mena** ("to mean, to hold the view"),
**hävda** ("to claim, to assert"), **påstå** ("to claim, to allege") and **framhålla**
("to emphasise, to point out"). These are followed by an **att-sats** (a subordinate
clause with "att"), where the familiar BIFF rule applies.

```example
Författaren menar att regeringen borde göra mer. — The author holds that the government ought to do more.
Hon hävdar att problemet är större än man tror. — She claims the problem is bigger than people think.
```

A source reference is built with **enligt** ("according to"): **enligt författaren**
("according to the author"), **enligt texten** ("according to the text"), **enligt
undersökningen** ("according to the study"). Another useful impersonal phrase is **det
framgår att...** ("it emerges that...", "it becomes clear that...").

```example
Enligt texten är arbetslösheten hög bland unga. — According to the text, unemployment is high among young people.
Det framgår av artikeln att situationen har förbättrats. — It emerges from the article that the situation has improved.
```

A referat is usually wrapped up with **sammanfattningsvis** ("in summary", "to sum up").
A good referat has no room for "jag tycker" or "jag tror" — only a neutral account of what
the **författare** ("author") wrote in the **artikel** ("article") or **källa** ("source").
