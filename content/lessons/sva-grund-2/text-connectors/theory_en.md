Within a single sentence, conjunctions like *och, men, eftersom* link clauses — you've
already seen those. But a connected text made of several sentences or paragraphs also
needs **text connectors** (linking adverbials) that show how whole ideas relate to each
other, not just how two verbs join inside one sats.

These are ordinary adverbials — so when a connector opens a sentence (sits in the
fundament), the **V2** rule applies: the subjekt and the verb swap places.

```example
Hon pluggar svenska. Dessutom jobbar hon deltid. — She's studying Swedish. In addition, she works part-time.
```

Notice: "Dessutom" takes the fundament, so the verb "jobbar" stays in second place and
the subjekt "hon" moves to third, exactly like with any other fronted adverbial.

"Däremot" (in contrast, on the other hand) behaves the same way and signals a contrast
with the previous sentence:

```example
Anna gillar att resa. Däremot stannar Erik hellre hemma. — Anna likes to travel. Erik, in contrast, prefers to stay home.
Jag har inte tid idag. Däremot är jag ledig imorgon. — I don't have time today. Tomorrow, in contrast, I'm free.
```

"Alltså" and "därför" introduce a consequence (= "therefore", "so"):

```example
Det regnade hela dagen. Alltså stannade vi hemma. — It rained all day. So we stayed home.
Hon missade bussen. Därför kom hon sent. — She missed the bus. That's why she arrived late.
```

"Å andra sidan" (on the other hand) introduces an opposing viewpoint, usually after one
opinion has already been stated:

```example
Lägenheten är billig. Å andra sidan ligger den långt från centrum. — The apartment is cheap. On the other hand, it's far from the centre.
```

Text structure builds on the same principle as splitting into paragraphs: **one idea,
one paragraph**, and connectors are the bridges between paragraphs that show the reader
how ideas relate — addition (dessutom), contrast (däremot, å andra sidan), or
consequence (alltså, därför). Without them a text falls apart into disconnected
sentences, even if each one is grammatically correct.
