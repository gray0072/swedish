A conditional sentence with **om** ("if") comes in two kinds, and the grammar differs
between them, even though both sound like a plain "if" in English.

## Real condition

A condition that can genuinely happen. The **om**-clause is in the present tense, and
the main clause uses **ska** or also the present tense:

```example
Om det regnar stannar jag hemma. — If it rains, I'll stay home.
Om jag har tid ska jag ringa dig. — If I have time, I'll call you.
```

## Unreal (hypothetical) condition

A condition that's unlikely to happen or directly contradicts reality right now. The
**om**-clause is in the **preterite**, and the main clause uses **skulle** + infinitive:

```example
Om jag hade tid skulle jag resa mer. — If I had time, I would travel more.
Om han visste svaret skulle han säga det. — If he knew the answer, he would say it.
```

Note: the preterite form (`hade`, `visste`) here doesn't mean "happened in the past" —
it signals precisely that the condition is unreal right now.

## Telling them apart

Compare this pair:

```example
Om jag har pengar köper jag boken. — If I have money, I'll buy the book. (real, could happen)
Om jag hade pengar skulle jag köpa boken. — If I had money, I would buy the book. (hypothetical, no money now)
```

The `om`-clause can also come after the main clause — the meaning stays the same:

```example
Jag stannar hemma om det regnar. — I'll stay home if it rains.
```

The only difference is word order: when the `om`-clause comes first, it occupies the
whole fundament, and V2 inversion follows in the main clause — like after any fundament.
