Some Swedish conjunctions never appear alone — they come in a matched pair,
one word before each of the two things being linked, with both parts on
equal footing.

## både … och — both … and

Links two things that are both simply true, side by side.

```example
Hon talar både svenska och engelska. — She speaks both Swedish and English.
```

## varken … eller — neither … nor

The negative twin of *både … och*: both things are false.

```example
Han har varken tid eller pengar. — He has neither time nor money.
```

## inte bara … utan också — not only … but also

Adds emphasis: the second element is presented as an extra, sometimes
unexpected addition to the first.

```example
Hon är inte bara duktig utan också modig. — She is not only capable but
also brave.
```

## antingen … eller — either … or

Offers a genuine choice between two options — unlike *både … och*, only one
of them holds.

```example
Vi kan antingen ta bussen eller cykla. — We can either take the bus or
cycle.
```

## A shared shape

In every pair, the two linked **led** (members) should be grammatically
parallel — two nouns, two verbs, two adjectives, or two clauses of the same
kind — which is what makes the sentence read smoothly.

```example
Hon är varken trött eller ledsen. — She is neither tired nor sad. (adj + adj)
Vi ska antingen resa till Spanien eller stanna hemma. — We'll either travel
to Spain or stay home. (verb phrase + verb phrase)
```

## In short

Each pair marks a different logical relationship — addition, double
negation, emphasis, or choice — but all four keep their two linked parts
grammatically matched.
