Many Swedish verbs are always followed by the same preposition, forming a fixed pair that's worth learning together, not separately.

```example
Jag tänker ofta på min familj. — I often think about my family.
Jag litar på dig, men han bryr sig inte om andras åsikter. — I trust you, but he doesn't care about other people's opinions.
```

Some of these pairs describe structure or content rather than feeling — useful for describing a course, a book, or a plan.

```example
Kursen består av tio lektioner. — The course consists of ten lessons.
Boken handlar om en resa till Norrland. — The book is about a trip to Norrland.
```

Others express dependence or a search:

```example
Resultatet är beroende av vädret. — The result depends on the weather.
Jag letar efter mina nycklar överallt. — I'm looking for my keys everywhere.
```

Because these prepositions rarely match a word-for-word translation, the safest approach is to memorise the verb and its preposition as one unit — *lita på*, not *lita* alone.
