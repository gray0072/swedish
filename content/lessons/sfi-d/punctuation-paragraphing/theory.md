The core Swedish comma rule: put a **komma** before a **bisats** (subordinate clause) when it follows the **huvudsats** (main clause) — unlike English, this comma is standard practice, not optional.

```example
Jag stannade hemma, eftersom det regnade. — I stayed home, since it was raining.
Hon sa att hon var trött, fast ingen trodde henne. — She said she was tired, although no one believed her.
```

When the subordinate clause comes first, the comma sits right before the main clause instead — the same rule, just applied to whichever clause comes second.

```example
Eftersom det regnade, stannade jag hemma. — Since it was raining, I stayed home.
```

Every sentence still ends with a **punkt** (full stop), question mark, or exclamation mark as usual.

For longer texts, good **styckeindelning** (paragraphing) matters as much as punctuation inside the sentence: each **stycke** (paragraph) should hold **en ny tanke** (one new idea), with a blank line or indent marking the shift.

```example
Nytt stycke, ny tanke — det gör texten lättare att följa. — New paragraph, new idea — it makes the text easier to follow.
```
