Five conjunctions link a time clause to the main sentence, each with its own shade. **När** (when) is the all-purpose one — a repeated habit or a general time.

```example
Jag ler när jag ser henne. — I smile when I see her.
```

**Då** (when) points at one specific moment in the past, often the start of the sentence in a story — many speakers now just use *när* here too, but *då* still sounds natural for this.

```example
Då jag var liten bodde vi i Umeå. — When I was little, we lived in Umeå.
```

**Medan** (while) marks two things happening at the same time; **innan** (before) and **efter att** (after) order two events; **så fort som** (as soon as) marks immediate sequence.

```example
Jag lagar mat medan du dukar. — I'm cooking while you set the table.
Ring mig innan du åker, och jag ringer så fort som jag är hemma. — Call me before you leave, and I'll call as soon as I'm home.
Efter att hon hade ätit gick hon och la sig. — After she had eaten, she went to bed.
```

Notice how **efter att** often pairs naturally with the pluperfect (*hade ätit*) in the subordinate clause, since it names something completed before the next action.
