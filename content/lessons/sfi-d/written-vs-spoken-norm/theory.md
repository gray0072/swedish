Standard written Swedish keeps a few distinctions that speech has largely dropped. **De** (subject: they) and **dem** (object: them) are grammatically different, but both are spoken as *dom* — the written distinction still matters when writing.

```example
De bor i Malmö, och jag såg dem på torget igår. — They live in Malmö, and I saw them at the square yesterday.
```

**Ska** is now the standard everyday form for "will/shall"; **skall** is older and mostly survives in **myndighetstext** (official/authority text) and law.

```example
Jag ska resa imorgon. — I will travel tomorrow. (everyday)
Ansökan skall vara inlämnad senast fredag. — The application shall be submitted by Friday at the latest. (formal/official)
```

Similarly, **sa** is the normal form of "said" in both speech and most writing today; **sade** is the older, more formal alternative, still common in careful or literary writing.

```example
Hon sa att hon kommer. — She said she's coming. (everyday)
Statsministern sade att läget var stabilt. — The Prime Minister said the situation was stable. (formal)
```

The pattern across all three pairs: pick the shorter, everyday form (*dom* in speech only, *ska*, *sa*) for ordinary writing and talk, and recognise the older form for reading official or formal texts.
