When telling a story with several past events, Swedish uses **pluskvamperfekt** (the pluperfect, *hade* + supine) to mark whichever event happened first — before the main preteritum action.

```example
När jag kom till stationen hade tåget redan gått. — When I got to the station, the train had already left.
Jag hade glömt nyckeln hemma, så jag fick ringa på hos grannen. — I had forgotten my key at home, so I had to ring the neighbour's bell.
```

The word **redan** (already) very often signals the pluperfect, and clauses with **innan** (before) or **när** (when) often set up exactly this two-step timeline: something had already happened by the time something else occurred.

```example
Regnet hade slutat innan vi gick ut. — The rain had stopped before we went out.
Hon hade ätit innan hon gick till jobbet. — She had eaten before she went to work.
```

Notice the difference from the simple preteritum: "Jag åt" (I ate) just states a past fact, while "Jag hade ätit" (I had eaten) places that fact clearly before another past moment — useful whenever you're **hinna** (managing, or failing) to do something in time.
