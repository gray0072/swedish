Much of everyday contact with Swedish authorities happens online, through **BankID** — an app on your phone that proves who you are. You use it to **logga in** (log in) to your bank, to **Mina sidor** ("My pages") at Försäkringskassan or Skatteverket, and to **1177** (the healthcare guidance service, both a phone number and a website/app).

```example
Jag loggar in med BankID på Mina sidor. — I log in with BankID on Mina sidor.
Jag ringer 1177 om jag är osäker på symtomen. — I call 1177 if I'm unsure about the symptoms.
```

To use most **e-tjänster** (e-services) you need an **e-legitimation** (electronic ID) — BankID is the most common one. Instead of typing a password on a shared computer, you often **skanna en QR-kod** with your phone, or **identifiera dig** directly in the app.

```example
Skanna QR-koden med kameran i BankID-appen. — Scan the QR code with the camera in the BankID app.
Du måste identifiera dig för att se dina uppgifter. — You must identify yourself to see your details.
```

Post from authorities can also arrive in a **digital brevlåda** (digital mailbox) instead of on paper — worth checking so you don't miss an important letter. If you forget your **lösenord** (password) or **användarnamn** (username), most services let you reset it, but with BankID there is usually no password to remember at all — just your phone and your code.
