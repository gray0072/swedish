Written Swedish and fast spoken Swedish can sound surprisingly different. **De** and **dem** (they/them) almost always become **dom** in speech, regardless of which one the writing would use.

```example
Dom kommer imorgon, men dom vet inte klockan. — They're coming tomorrow, but they don't know the time.
```

**Och** (and) is often reduced to just **å**, **jag** (I) can shrink to **ja**, and **sedan** (then) to **sen** — small, very frequent words wear down the fastest in **reduktion** (reduction).

```example
Ja å du kan gå tillsammans, å sen äter vi. — You and I can go together, and then we'll eat.
```

**Någon** (someone) often becomes **nån**, and if you miss something entirely, the short, very common way to ask someone to repeat is simply **va?** (what?).

```example
Är det nån hemma? — Va sa du? — Is anyone home? — What did you say?
```

None of these reduced forms belong in formal writing — **skriftspråket** (the written language) keeps the full forms — but recognising them is essential for understanding real, everyday speech.
