A concessive clause names something that would normally stop or prevent the main action — but didn't. **Fast** is the everyday, spoken word for "although"; **trots att** is a bit more formal; **även om** means "even if" and covers both real and hypothetical concessions.

```example
Jag gick ut, fast det regnade. — I went out, although it was raining.
Hon log trots att hon var trött. — She smiled despite being tired.
Jag kommer, även om jag är sjuk. — I'll come, even if I'm sick.
```

**Trots** (without *att*) is a preposition, used directly before a noun rather than a clause.

```example
Trots regnet gick de ut och promenerade. — Despite the rain, they went out for a walk.
```

Two useful companions: **ändå** (still, nevertheless) often appears in the main clause to underline the contrast, and **visserligen** (admittedly) opens a sentence that concedes a point before contradicting it.

```example
Det regnade, men vi gick ut ändå. — It was raining, but we went out anyway.
Visserligen är det dyrt, men det håller länge. — Admittedly it's expensive, but it lasts a long time.
```
