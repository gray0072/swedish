Every spring, **Skatteverket** (the Tax Agency) sends everyone a **deklaration** (tax declaration). For most employees it sounds scarier than it is: your **arbetsgivare** (employer) has already reported your **inkomst** (income), so the numbers are pre-filled — you usually only need to check them and **godkänna** (approve) the form, often with BankID in minutes.

```example
Jag godkänner min deklaration med BankID på fem minuter. — I approve my declaration with BankID in five minutes.
Arbetsgivaren rapporterar din lön till Skatteverket. — Your employer reports your pay to the Tax Agency.
```

If you have extra **avdrag** (deductions) to claim — for example a long commute — you add them yourself before approving. There is a **sista dag** (final deadline), usually in May, so don't leave it too late.

```example
Sista dag att deklarera är i maj. — The final deadline to file is in May.
Reseavdrag är ett vanligt avdrag att lägga till. — A travel deduction is a common one to add.
```

Afterwards you find out whether you get **skatteåterbäring** (a tax refund, paid out in summer) or must pay **kvarskatt** (residual tax still owed). Either way, for someone with a simple, single income, "deklarera" mostly means reading carefully and clicking approve.
