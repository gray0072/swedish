**Direkt tal** (direct speech) quotes the exact words; **indirekt tal** (reported speech) folds them into a subordinate clause with **att** (that), after a verb like **säga** (say) or **påstå** (claim).

```example
"Jag är trött", sa hon. — direkt tal. — "I'm tired," she said. — direct speech.
Hon sa att hon var trött. — indirekt tal. — She said that she was tired. — reported speech.
```

A yes/no question has no question word to carry into the subordinate clause, so Swedish uses **om** (whether) instead, after verbs like **fråga** (ask) or **undra** (wonder).

```example
"Är du hungrig?" → Han frågade om jag var hungrig. — "Are you hungry?" → He asked whether I was hungry.
"Kommer hon idag?" → Jag undrar om hon kommer idag. — "Is she coming today?" → I wonder whether she's coming today.
```

Notice the word order: the subordinate clause keeps straight (BIFF) word order, with the verb after the subject, unlike the inverted order of the original question.

```example
Jag vet inte om han kommer i tid. — I don't know whether he'll arrive on time.
```
