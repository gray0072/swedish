The imperative — used for instructions, recipes, and signs — is formed differently by verb group, though it always ends up as the shortest form of the verb.

**Group 1** (-ar in present) drops -r: infinitive *titta* → imperative **titta** (same as infinitive, since it already ends in -a).

```example
Titta här! Koka potatisen i tio minuter. — Look here! Boil the potatoes for ten minutes.
```

**Group 2** and **group 4** strip the ending down to the bare stem — often identical to the present tense without the -r.

```example
Ring mig ikväll! Stäng dörren, tack. — Call me tonight! Close the door, please.
Skriv ditt namn här. — Write your name here.
```

**Group 3** (short verbs ending in a stressed vowel) keeps the infinitive unchanged as the imperative.

```example
Tro på dig själv! — Believe in yourself!
```

Summary:

| group | infinitive | imperative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | titta | titta |
| 2 | ringa / stänga | ring / stäng |
| 3 | tro | tro |
| 4 | skriva | skriv |

This is exactly why signs, recipes and instructions ("Följ receptet", "Öppna dörren") sound so terse — the imperative is always the shortest possible form of the verb.
