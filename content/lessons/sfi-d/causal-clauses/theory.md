Three ways to give a reason, in decreasing order of formality: **eftersom** (since/as) often opens the sentence and states a reason the listener may not know yet; **därför att** (because) is the everyday choice, often answering a "why" question directly.

```example
Eftersom det regnade stannade vi hemma. — Since it was raining, we stayed home.
Varför stannade du hemma? — Därför att det regnade. — Why did you stay home? — Because it was raining.
```

**På grund av** (due to) is a preposition, so it takes a noun, not a full clause — handy for a short, formal reason.

```example
Tåget var försenat på grund av snön. — The train was delayed due to the snow.
```

Don't confuse **därför att** (because, joining two clauses) with **därför** (therefore, an adverb that starts a new sentence stating the result) — they point the logic in opposite directions.

```example
Det regnade, därför stannade vi hemma. — It was raining, therefore we stayed home. (result)
Vi stannade hemma därför att det regnade. — We stayed home because it was raining. (reason)
```

To ask about the cause directly, use **orsak** (reason/cause) or the verb **bero på** (to be due to).

```example
Förseningen beror på vädret. — The delay is due to the weather.
```
