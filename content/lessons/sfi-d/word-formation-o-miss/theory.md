The prefix **o-** negates an adjective, turning it into its opposite — much like English "un-" or "im-".

```example
möjlig → omöjlig — possible → impossible
Det är omöjligt att hinna dit i tid. — It's impossible to get there in time.
trevlig → otrevlig — pleasant → unpleasant
Han var otrevlig mot personalen. — He was unfriendly to the staff.
```

**O-** is very productive: it attaches to most descriptive adjectives — *vanlig* (usual) → *ovanlig* (unusual), *lycklig* (happy) → *olycklig* (unhappy) — and you can often guess an unfamiliar o-word's meaning if you know the base adjective.

```example
Det är ett ovanligt namn. — It's an unusual name.
```

The prefix **miss-** attaches mainly to verbs and nouns, adding the sense of "wrongly" or "failed" — a mistaken or unsuccessful version of the action.

```example
Jag missförstod hela meningen. — I misunderstood the whole sentence.
Projektet misslyckades trots allt arbete. — The project failed despite all the work.
```

The related noun **misstag** (mistake) makes the pattern explicit: something done *miss*- (wrongly), as a *tag* (a taking/an act).
