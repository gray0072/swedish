Swedish has five tenses that together describe any point in time. **Presens** (present) covers now and habits; **preteritum** (preterite) covers a completed past event; **futurum** (future) covers what will happen.

```example
Jag arbetar på ett kontor. — I work in an office. (presens)
Jag arbetade i Göteborg förra året. — I worked in Gothenburg last year. (preteritum)
Jag ska arbeta hemifrån imorgon. — I'm going to work from home tomorrow. (futurum)
```

Two more tenses are built with a **hjälpverb** (auxiliary verb) plus the **supinum** (supine): **perfekt** (perfect, with *har*) links the past to now, and **pluskvamperfekt** (pluperfect, with *hade*) marks something that happened even earlier than another past event.

```example
Jag har arbetat här i fem år. — I have worked here for five years. (perfekt — still true now)
Jag hade redan arbetat i tre år när jag flyttade hit. — I had already worked for three years when I moved here. (pluskvamperfekt — before another past point)
```

Here is the full system side by side:

| tense | built with | example |
|---|---|---|
| presens | verb stem + ending | arbetar |
| preteritum | verb stem + past ending | arbetade |
| perfekt | har + supinum | har arbetat |
| pluskvamperfekt | hade + supinum | hade arbetat |
| futurum | ska/kommer att + infinitive | ska arbeta |

Choosing the right tense is mostly about choosing the right relationship to "now" — and, for the past two, about ordering two past events correctly.
