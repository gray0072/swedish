It's natural to **jämföra** (compare) life in Sweden with life back home, and to notice both **skillnad** (difference) and **likhet** (similarity). Everyday **vana** (habits) — mealtimes, small talk, punctuality — often differ more than big things do.

```example
Den största skillnaden är tempot, men det finns också likheter. — The biggest difference is the pace, but there are also similarities.
```

**Förväntning** (expectations) around **artighet** (politeness/etiquette) can catch newcomers off guard — for example, a stronger sense of **personligt avstånd** (personal space) in a queue, or fewer small compliments in daily talk. This can feel like a small **kulturkrock** (culture clash) at first.

```example
Personligt avstånd i kön kändes konstigt först, men jag har vant mig. — Personal space in queues felt strange at first, but I've gotten used to it.
```

Over time you **vänja sig vid** (get used to) most new habits — even if you still **sakna** (miss) things from home, like certain food or a different way of greeting people.
