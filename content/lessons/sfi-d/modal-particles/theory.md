Small particles like **ju**, **nog**, **väl** and **faktiskt** add almost no dictionary meaning but colour the whole sentence — Swedish uses them constantly in speech.

**Ju** points at something the listener already knows or should agree with — like saying "you know" or "after all".

```example
Det är ju söndag idag, så affärerna är stängda. — It's Sunday today, you know, so the shops are closed.
```

**Nog** softens a statement into a likely guess — "probably" — without full commitment.

```example
Det blir nog regn imorgon. — It will probably rain tomorrow.
```

**Väl** invites agreement or expects confirmation, often turning a statement into something close to a tag question ("...right?").

```example
Du kommer väl imorgon? — You're coming tomorrow, aren't you?
```

**Faktiskt** signals that what follows might be surprising or contrary to expectation — "actually, in fact".

```example
Jag gillar faktiskt inte kaffe, fast alla tror det. — I actually don't like coffee, even though everyone assumes I do.
```

These words are optional grammatically but essential for sounding natural — they're what makes a sentence feel spoken rather than translated.
