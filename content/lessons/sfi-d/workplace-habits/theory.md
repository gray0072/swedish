Swedish workplaces have a few habits that surprise many newcomers. **Du-tilltal** (addressing people with the informal "du") is used with almost everyone, including your manager — titles and formal address are rare. Workplaces are often a **platt organisation** (flat organisation), aiming for **konsensus** (consensus) rather than one person simply deciding.

```example
På jobbet säger alla "du" till varandra, även till chefen. — At work everyone says "du" to each other, even to the boss.
Vi försöker nå konsensus innan vi bestämmer något. — We try to reach consensus before deciding anything.
```

**Fika** — a shared coffee break, usually mid-morning and mid-afternoon — is not just a pause but a small social ritual worth joining. **Punktlighet** (punctuality) matters: it's expected that you **komma i tid** (arrive on time), for a shift or for a meeting.

```example
Vi tar fika klockan tio, kom gärna med! — We take fika at ten, feel free to join!
Det är viktigt att komma i tid till mötet. — It's important to arrive on time for the meeting.
```

At the same time, you're expected to be **självständig** (independent) — but that doesn't mean staying silent when stuck: it's completely normal to **be om hjälp** (ask for help), and **rak kommunikation** (direct, straightforward communication) is generally appreciated over hinting.
