Almost every Swedish authority starts with the same question: what is your **personnummer** (personal identity number)? It has ten digits (birth date + four more) and follows you for life. Before you get one, you may first receive a **samordningsnummer** (coordination number) — a temporary alternative used until you are **folkbokförd** (registered as resident).

```example
Du behöver ett personnummer för det mesta i Sverige. — You need a personnummer for almost everything in Sweden.
Jag är folkbokförd i Malmö sedan i våras. — I've been registered as resident in Malmö since this spring.
```

**Folkbokföring** (population registration) records where you live, and decides which kommun you belong to and where you pay tax. If you move, you must **anmäla flytt** (report the move) — normally within a week — which is often the same as an **adressändring** (change of address).

```example
Gör en adressändring så fort du har flyttat. — File a change of address as soon as you've moved.
Anmäl flytt till Skatteverket inom en vecka. — Report your move to Skatteverket within a week.
```

To **styrka sin identitet** (prove your identity) you show a **pass** (passport) or an **id-kort** (ID card) — useful even without a driving licence, for example when picking up a parcel. Other official facts about you include your **medborgarskap** (citizenship), your **uppehållstillstånd** (residence permit, if you are not an EU citizen) and your **civilstånd** (marital status).
