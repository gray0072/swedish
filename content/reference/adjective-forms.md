# Adjective forms and agreement

A Swedish adjective changes its ending to agree with the noun it describes — by gender, by
number, and by whether the phrase is definite or indefinite.

## The three basic shapes

| | en-word | ett-word | plural (either gender) |
|---|---|---|---|
| indefinite | en stor bil | ett stort hus | stora bilar/hus |

```example
en stor bil — a big car
ett stort hus — a big house
stora bilar — big cars
```

The pattern is regular for most adjectives: **base form** for en-words, **base + t** for
ett-words, **base + a** for the plural.

## The definite form: always -a

After a definite article word (`den, det, de`) or a possessive, the adjective takes the
**same -a ending regardless of gender or number** — the en/ett distinction disappears entirely
in the definite:

```example
den stora bilen — the big car
det stora huset — the big house
de stora bilarna/husen — the big cars/houses
min stora bil — my big car
```

This is a genuine simplification compared to the indefinite forms above — one ending covers
every case once definiteness enters the picture.

## Irregulars worth memorising early

A handful of very common adjectives do not follow the regular -t/-a pattern and have to be
learned as a set:

| Base | ett-form | Plural / definite | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| liten | litet | **små** (plural), **lilla** (definite sg.) | small |
| gammal | gammalt | gamla | old |
| ny | nytt | nya | new (regular, shown for contrast) |

```example
en liten hund — a small dog
ett litet hus — a small house
små hundar/hus — small dogs/houses
den lilla hunden — the small dog (definite singular uses "lilla", not "lilla" from "små")
```

`liten` is the single most irregular adjective in everyday Swedish: four different shapes for
four different grammatical slots, where a regular adjective needs only two. See
`liten-agreement` for a full drill.

## Adjectives that never change

A small set of adjectives, mostly borrowed or ending in a vowel already, take no ending at
all: **bra**, **fel**, **öppen**'s cousin **öppet**-invariant forms like **lila** (purple),
**rosa** (pink), **beige**. `en bra bil`, `ett bra hus`, `bra bilar` — identical throughout.
