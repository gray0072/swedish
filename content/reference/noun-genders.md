# Noun genders: en / ett, and the definite form

Every Swedish noun has a gender — **en** or **ett**. Roughly 75% of nouns are en-words, so
guessing "en" when unsure is not the worst bet — but there are enough exceptions that gender
should be learned together with the word, not derived from a rule.

## Indefinite and definite form

Unlike Russian, Swedish has no separate word for "the": definiteness is expressed by a
**suffix** glued directly onto the noun.

| | en-word (**bok**, book) | ett-word (**hus**, house) |
|---|---|---|
| indefinite ("a book") | en bok | ett hus |
| definite ("the book") | bok**en** | hus**et** |

```example
en bok — a book
boken — the book
```

## Plural

Plural endings are not uniform — there are five productive patterns, and which one a noun
takes is, again, something to memorise with the word rather than to predict.

| Pattern | Example | Indefinite plural | Definite plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-or** (mostly en-words ending in -a) | flicka (girl) | flick**or** | flick**orna** |
| **-ar** (many en-words) | bil (car) | bil**ar** | bil**arna** |
| **-er** | familj (family) | familj**er** | familj**erna** |
| **no ending** (many ett-words) | hus (house) | hus | hus**en** |
| **-n** (ett-words ending in a vowel) | äpple (apple) | äpple**n** | äpple**na** |

The definite plural is more predictable than it looks: it is almost always the indefinite
plural + **-na** (or **-en**, when the stem already ends in a consonant right against the
ending). See `noun-forms` for the full four-form table and the irregular plurals that break
this pattern outright (`man` → `män`, `barn` → `barn`).

## What predicts gender, loosely

There is no rule that always works, but these tendencies help:

- Words for people and living things are almost always **en**: en pojke, en lärare, en hund.
- Words ending in **-a**, **-dom**, **-else**, **-het**, **-ing**, **-ion** are almost always
  **en**: en flicka, en sjukdom, en känsla, en frihet, en tidning, en station.
- Nouns formed from a verb infinitive by dropping the final **-a** are almost always **ett**:
  *(att) springa* → **ett** spring; *(att) skriva* → **ett** skrivande takes a different suffix,
  but the pattern still leans **ett**.
- Metals, letters of the alphabet, and most one-syllable loanwords are frequently **ett**:
  ett guld, ett a, ett kilo.

None of these are guarantees — `ett hus`, `ett bord`, `ett barn` have no ending clue at all.

## Adjectives agree with gender

Adjectives — including colours — change their ending to match the gender of the noun they
describe: **en röd bil**, but **ett rött hus**, and in the plural, **röda bilar**. The full
agreement table, and the irregular adjectives that do not follow it, live in
`adjective-forms`.
