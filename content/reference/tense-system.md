# The tense system: which one, and why

Swedish has the same forms as `verb-forms` describes, but the hard part is not building the
form — it is choosing between **preteritum** and **perfekt**, which Russian's aspect system and
English's tense system both carve up differently.

## Preteritum vs. perfekt

Both can translate as a Russian past tense or an English simple past, but they answer different
questions:

| | Preteritum | Perfekt |
|---|---|---|
| Answers | *When* did it happen? | *Has* it happened — is it relevant now? |
| Needs | a specific point or period in time | no specific time at all |
| Example | Jag åt frukost klockan sju. | Jag har ätit frukost. (so I'm not hungry now) |

```example
Jag flyttade till Sverige 2019. — I moved to Sweden in 2019. (a dated fact: preteritum)
Jag har bott i Sverige i fem år. — I have lived in Sweden for five years. (still true: perfekt)
```

The test that works in practice: if the sentence names or implies *when* something happened
(`i går`, `2019`, `förra veckan`), use preteritum. If it only says *that* something has
happened and the moment doesn't matter, use perfekt. Swedish news headlines lean heavily on
perfekt for exactly this reason — the event's relevance now, not its date, is the point.

## Pluskvamperfekt: the past before the past

Used the same way as English "had done" — for an event that was already finished by the time
another past event happened:

```example
När jag kom hem hade hon redan lagat mat. — When I got home, she had already cooked.
```

See `pluperfect-narrative` for a full story told in this tense.

## The future: no dedicated future tense

Swedish has no separate future verb form. Three constructions cover it instead, each with a
different shade of meaning:

| Construction | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **presens** | a scheduled, near-certain event | Tåget går klockan åtta. (The train leaves at eight.) |
| **ska** + infinitive | an intention or a plan | Jag ska resa till Spanien i sommar. |
| **kommer att** + infinitive | a prediction, something expected to happen | Det kommer att regna i morgon. |

```example
Jag ska laga middag ikväll. — I'm going to cook dinner tonight. (my plan)
Det kommer att bli kallt i helgen. — It's going to be cold this weekend. (a forecast)
```
