# Adverbs

Most Swedish adverbs are built from adjectives, but the language also has a set of adverb
*pairs* that neither Russian nor English marks the way Swedish does — and those pairs are the
part actually worth studying.

## From adjective to adverb: add -t

The regular pattern reuses the adjective's ett-form as the adverb — the same **-t** ending
already seen in `adjective-forms`:

```example
snabb (fast, adj.) → Han kör snabbt. (He drives fast.)
tydlig (clear, adj.) → Förklara tydligt! (Explain clearly!)
```

Because this is the same form as the ett-agreement adjective, context is what tells them apart:
`ett snabbt tåg` (a fast train, adjective) versus `Han kör snabbt` (he drives fast, adverb).

## Where adverbs sit in the sentence

Adverbs of manner and sentence adverbs (`inte, ofta, alltid, kanske`) follow the position rules
from `word-order`: after the finite verb in a main clause, before it in a subordinate clause
(the BIFF rule).

```example
Jag springer ofta på morgonen. — I often run in the morning.
Jag vet att hon alltid kommer i tid. — I know she always arrives on time.
```

## Place adverbs that come in pairs: motion vs. location

This is the genuinely new idea for a Russian or English speaker: Swedish uses a **different
word** depending on whether something is moving toward a place or already there — a
distinction neither language marks this explicitly outside of a few fossilised pairs like
English "home"/"at home".

| Motion (where to?) | Location (where at?) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| hem | hemma | home |
| dit | där | there |
| hit | här | here |
| ut | ute | out |
| in | inne | in |
| upp | uppe | up |
| ner | nere | down |

```example
Jag ska gå hem. — I'm going home. (motion)
Jag är hemma. — I'm at home. (location)
Kom hit! — Come here! (motion)
Jag väntar här. — I'm waiting here. (location)
```

Mixing these up is one of the most common intermediate-level errors, precisely because a
learner's first language usually offers only one word where Swedish insists on two.

## Frequency and degree adverbs worth knowing early

`alltid` (always), `ofta` (often), `ibland` (sometimes), `sällan` (rarely), `aldrig` (never),
`ganska` (fairly), `mycket` (very), `nästan` (almost) — these carry a large share of everyday
conversation and, being invariant, are among the easiest words in the language once memorised.
