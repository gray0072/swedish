# Verb groups 1–4

Swedish verbs fall into four groups by how they form the past tense and the supine (the form
needed for compound tenses). In the present tense the difference barely shows — almost every
verb simply adds **-r** (see `verb-forms` for the whole system).

| Group | Infinitive | Present | Past | Supine | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | -a | -ar | -ade | -at | tala → talar → talade → talat (to speak) |
| **2** | -a / consonant | -er | -de / -te | -t | ringa → ringer → ringde → ringt (to call) |
| **3** | vowel (not -a) | -r | -dde | -tt | bo → bor → bodde → bott (to live) |
| **4** (strong) | -a | -er | vowel change | -it | skriva → skriver → skrev → skrivit (to write) |

## Group 1 — the largest and the most predictable

If a verb's infinitive ends in **-a** and is not one of the exceptions, it is almost certainly
group 1. It is also the productive group: new loanwords (**maila**, "to email") automatically
conjugate this way.

```example
arbeta — arbetar — arbetade — arbetat (to work)
studera — studerar — studerade — studerat (to study)
```

## Group 2 — split by a spelling rule

Group 2 splits into 2a and 2b depending on what the stem ends in: after a voiced consonant
(most cases) the past ends in **-de**; after an unvoiced consonant (`k, p, s, t, x`) it ends in
**-te** instead — the spelling follows the pronunciation.

```example
ringa — ringer — ringde — ringt (to call, voiced stem)
läsa — läser — läste — läst (to read, unvoiced stem)
```

## Group 3 — short and vowel-final

Verbs whose infinitive already ends in a stressed vowel other than **-a** (bo, tro, sy, må)
double the final consonant of the ending instead of adding one.

```example
tro — tror — trodde — trott (to believe)
```

## Group 4 — the "irregular" verbs

Group 4 is the strong verbs, where the past tense is formed by **changing the root vowel**
instead of adding an ending — much like English irregular verbs (write → wrote) or Russian's
own root alternations. They cannot be derived by rule, only learned — but `strong-verbs`
groups them by vowel pattern, which makes a hundred of them far more memorable than a flat
list.

```example
äta — äter — åt — ätit (to eat)
dricka — dricker — drack — druckit (to drink)
```

## The true exceptions

A handful of the most frequent verbs in the language belong to no group cleanly: **vara**
("to be") → är, var, varit; **vilja** ("to want") → vill, ville, velat; **kunna** ("can, to be
able") → kan, kunde, kunnat. Learn these first, and learn them separately.
