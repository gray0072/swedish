# Comparison: komparativ and superlativ

Swedish adjectives compare the way English ones do — either with an ending or with a separate
word — and a handful of very common adjectives are irregular in exactly the way their English
translations are.

## The regular pattern: -are / -ast

Most adjectives add **-are** for the comparative and **-ast** for the superlative:

```example
stor — större — störst (big — bigger — biggest)
snabb — snabbare — snabbast (fast — faster — fastest)
```

## The periphrastic pattern: mer / mest

Longer adjectives, especially those ending in **-isk**, **-ad**, or a present/past participle,
use **mer** ("more") and **mest** ("most") instead of an ending — exactly parallel to English
"more interesting / most interesting":

```example
intressant — mer intressant — mest intressant (interesting)
komplicerad — mer komplicerad — mest komplicerad (complicated)
```

## Irregular comparatives

A short list of frequent adjectives compare irregularly, and — usefully for a Russian or
English speaker — several of them are irregular in a familiar-feeling way:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| bra / god | bättre | bäst | good |
| dålig | sämre | värst | bad |
| liten | mindre | minst | small |
| stor | större | störst | big |
| gammal | äldre | äldst | old |
| många | fler | flest | many |

```example
Den här boken är bättre än den där. — This book is better than that one.
Hon har flest böcker av oss alla. — She has the most books of all of us.
```

## The definite superlative: -aste / -a

In the definite (after `den/det/de`), the superlative usually adds **-e** or **-a** rather
than the bare **-ast**/**-t** ending:

```example
den snabbaste bilen — the fastest car
den bästa lösningen — the best solution
```

## "than" and "as … as"

Comparisons use **än** for "than" and **lika … som** for "as … as":

```example
Han är längre än sin bror. — He is taller than his brother.
Hon är lika gammal som jag. — She is as old as I am.
```
