# Modal verbs

Eight verbs carry most of Swedish's talk about ability, permission, obligation and intention.
All of them are followed by a **bare infinitive** — no `att` ("to"), unlike most other Swedish
verb + infinitive pairs (see `infinitive-constructions`).

| Modal | Core meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ska** | intention, a plan, an instruction | Jag ska ringa henne. — I'm going to call her. |
| **vill** | want | Vill du ha kaffe? — Do you want coffee? |
| **kan** | ability, possibility | Kan du simma? — Can you swim? |
| **måste** | necessity, must | Jag måste gå nu. — I have to go now. |
| **får** | permission, "may" | Får jag fråga en sak? — May I ask something? |
| **bör** | recommendation, "should" | Du bör vila. — You should rest. |
| **brukar** | habitual action, "usually" | Jag brukar jogga på morgonen. — I usually jog in the morning. |
| **skulle** | conditional, hypothetical, polite request | Skulle du kunna hjälpa mig? — Could you help me? |

## The bare infinitive after a modal

```example
Jag kan simma. — I can swim.
Vi måste gå. — We have to go.
```

No `att` appears before `simma` or `gå` — this is what makes modals structurally distinct from
a verb like `försöka` ("to try"), which does take `att`: `Jag försöker att simma`.

## brukar vs. ska vs. kommer att

Learners often reach for `ska` to describe a habit, but Swedish reserves that job for
`brukar`:

```example
Jag brukar äta lunch klockan tolv. — I usually eat lunch at twelve. (habit)
Jag ska äta lunch klockan tolv idag. — I'm going to eat lunch at twelve today. (a specific plan)
```

## skulle: the politeness modal

`skulle` — the preteritum of `ska` — is Swedish's main tool for softening a request or a
statement into something hypothetical or polite, structurally close to English "would" or
"could":

```example
Skulle du kunna stänga fönstret? — Could you close the window?
Jag skulle vilja ha en kaffe, tack. — I would like a coffee, please.
```

`skulle-infinitive-politeness` and `infinitive-constructions` cover this pattern, and the wider
family of verb + infinitive pairs, in more depth.
