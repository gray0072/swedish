# Numbers, time and dates

## Cardinal and ordinal numbers

```example
1 en/ett, 2 två, 3 tre, 4 fyra, 5 fem, 6 sex, 7 sju, 8 åtta, 9 nio, 10 tio
1:a första, 2:a andra, 3:e tredje, 4:e fjärde, 5:e femte
```

`en`/`ett` for "one" follows the same en/ett gender split as nouns (`noun-genders`): **en bil**
(one car), **ett hus** (one house). Ordinals are written with a colon and the last letter of
the word (`3:e`, `21:a`) and take an adjective-like -e/-a ending depending on gender and
definiteness. See `numbers-0-20` for the full cardinal list from 0–20 and `numbers-personal-details`
for how numbers combine into personal ID and phone numbers.

## Telling time — the "half past the next hour" trap

Swedish tells the half hour by naming the **coming** hour, not the one just passed — the
opposite of how a Russian or English speaker's instinct runs after translating literally:

```example
klockan åtta — eight o'clock
kvart över åtta — quarter past eight
halv nio — half past eight (literally "half nine" — points to the NEXT hour)
kvart i nio — quarter to nine
```

`halv nio` genuinely means 8:30, not 9:30 — this single point causes more scheduling
misunderstandings than any other piece of Swedish grammar. See `clock` for a full drill.

## Dates

Day, then month, then year, written with periods, and the year is not abbreviated in formal
writing:

```example
den 15 september 2026 — 15 September 2026
15/9 2026 — 15/9 2026 (short form, day before month — the reverse of US usage)
```

`writing-dates` covers the full pattern including how to say a date aloud, which uses the
ordinal: `den femtonde september` ("the fifteenth of September").

## Money and phone numbers

Prices are read as plain cardinal numbers followed by the currency, and phone numbers are
usually read digit by digit or in pairs, not as one long number:

```example
149 kronor — a hundra fyrtionio kronor
070-123 45 67 — noll sjuttio, etthundratjugotre, fyrtiofem, sextiosju
```

`phone-numbers` and `bill-receipt-payslip` cover reading real Swedish documents that use these
patterns — a payslip, a receipt, a phone number on a form.
