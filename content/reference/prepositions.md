# Prepositions

Swedish prepositions map onto Russian cases and English prepositions inconsistently enough
that they are best learned as fixed phrases, not translated word by word.

## i vs. på — the two hardest to tell apart

Both can mean "in" or "at" in English, and Swedish's split does not track any rule a Russian or
English speaker can derive — it has to be learned per expression.

| Use | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| inside an enclosed space | i | i huset, i Sverige, i klassrummet |
| a surface, or an institution treated as a place you attend | på | på bordet, på jobbet, på universitetet |
| days of the week | på | på måndag |
| months, years, seasons | i | i januari, i år, i somras |

```example
Jag bor i Stockholm. — I live in Stockholm.
Jag jobbar på ett sjukhus. — I work at a hospital.
```

See `prepositions-i-pa` and `pa-with-days` for a focused drill on this split, and
`abstract-prepositions` for how the same two extend into non-physical meanings
(`i genomsnitt` — on average; `på riktigt` — for real).

## till, hos, vid

- **till** — motion toward a destination: `Jag åker till jobbet.` (I'm going to work.)
- **hos** — "at someone's place", a person rather than a location: `Jag är hos min mormor.`
  (I'm at my grandmother's.) Russian's «у меня» has no single-word Swedish match; `hos` is the
  closest, and only for people.
- **vid** — "by/near", a fixed point: `Huset ligger vid sjön.` (The house is by the lake.)

## Time prepositions worth memorising as a set

```example
klockan tre — at three o'clock
om en vecka — in a week (from now)
för en vecka sedan — a week ago
sedan 2019 — since 2019
under sommaren — during the summer
```

## Verb + preposition pairs

Many Swedish verbs pair with a specific preposition that does not translate literally and has
to be memorised with the verb, the way English "look **forward to**" or "depend **on**" do:

```example
tycka om — to like ("think about/around")
titta på — to watch/look at
vänta på — to wait for
lyssna på — to listen to
```

`verb-preposition-pairs` covers the full set used across the curriculum; treat each pair as one
vocabulary item, not two words to translate separately.
