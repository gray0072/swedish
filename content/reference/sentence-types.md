# Sentence types

Four sentence shapes cover almost everything a learner needs to build, and each behaves
differently under the V2 rule (§ `word-order`).

## Statement (påstående)

Subject-first is the default order; anything else in first position pushes the verb-subject
pair to swap (§ `word-order`).

```example
Jag jobbar hemma idag. — I'm working from home today.
Idag jobbar jag hemma. — Today I'm working from home.
```

## Yes/no question

The finite verb simply moves to first position — nothing else changes.

```example
Jobbar du hemma idag? — Are you working from home today?
```

## Question-word question

A question word (`vem, vad, var, när, hur, varför, vilken`) takes first position, and the verb
stays second, exactly as it does in a statement.

```example
Var jobbar du idag? — Where are you working today?
Varför jobbar du hemma? — Why are you working from home?
```

## Subordinate clause (bisats)

Introduced by a subjunktion (`att, om, när, eftersom, fastän`…) or a relative pronoun (`som`),
a subordinate clause cannot stand alone, and it reorders its own sentence adverbs: `inte` and
similar words move **before** the finite verb instead of after it (the BIFF rule, in
`word-order`).

```example
Jag vet att hon inte kommer idag. — I know that she isn't coming today.
Mannen som bor här är lärare. — The man who lives here is a teacher.
```

## Imperative

The bare stem of the verb, with the subject dropped entirely — Swedish has no equivalent of
English *"you"* in a command.

```example
Kom hit! — Come here!
Stäng dörren, tack. — Close the door, please.
```

See `imperative-four-groups` for how the stem is derived from each of the four verb groups,
and `pronouns` for `man`, the impersonal pronoun Swedish uses instead of a generic "you" in
statements ("one does this" rather than "you do this").
