# Strong verbs, by vowel pattern

Group-4 verbs (`verb-groups`) form their past tense by changing the root vowel instead of
adding an ending, and there is no rule that predicts which change applies to which verb. What
does help: the roughly hundred strong verbs a learner needs cluster into a handful of
**vowel patterns**, and learning the pattern is far more memorable than a flat alphabetical
list.

## The main patterns

| Pattern (inf–past–supine) | Verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **i – e – i** | skriva, vika, bita, rida, skina | skriva → skrev → skrivit (write) |
| **i – a – u** | vinna, brinna, försvinna, hinna | vinna → vann → vunnit (win) |
| **ju/u – ö – u** | bjuda, njuta, flyga → flög, sluta* | bjuda → bjöd → bjudit (invite) |
| **y/u – ö – u** | flyta, njuta, sluta, frysa | frysa → frös → frusit (freeze) |
| **ä – a – u** | bära, skära, stjäla | bära → bar → burit (carry) |
| **a – o – a** | fara, ta(ga), dra(ga), slå | fara → for → farit (travel) |
| **i – e – e** (short) | ge, se, vara(*irregular) | ge → gav → gett (give) |

*(a few verbs sit at the edge of a pattern rather than inside it cleanly — the table groups
them by what is useful to learn together, not by strict etymology.)*

```example
Han skrev ett brev och har nu skrivit tre. — He wrote a letter and has now written three.
Vi bjöd honom på middag. — We invited him for dinner.
Hon bar väskan hela vägen. — She carried the bag the whole way.
```

## Why pattern beats list

A learner who has drilled `skriva/skrev/skrivit` can produce `vika/vek/vikit` (to fold) on the
first try, because the vowel shift **i → e → i** is now a shape, not a fact about one verb.
This is the same logic English speakers use for *sing/sang/sung*, *ring/rang/rung*,
*swim/swam/swum* — a pattern recognised once, applied many times.

## The irregulars that fit no pattern

A small set of very frequent verbs are strong but idiosyncratic enough to learn on their own:
`gå → gick → gått` (go), `göra → gjorde → gjort` (do/make), `komma → kom → kommit` (come),
`se → såg → sett` (see). High frequency is exactly why they resist being smoothed into a
pattern — irregularity survives in a language precisely in its most-used words.
